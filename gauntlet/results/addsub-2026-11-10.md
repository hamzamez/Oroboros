# Algorithms A and S, each its own loop (items 3 and 5, and the index split)

Date: 2026-11-10. Items 3 and 5 of the standing measure's list (digitfacts-2026-11-10), with the
index domain split item 2 left owed. All three were in fib's addition loop, `addsub`:
- the operand swap and σ selected per digit;
- the size tracked per digit;
- a bounds test on every read, since the loop read past an operand's size.

## The derivation

- **The swap matters only for a difference.** x + y = y + x, so when σ = +1 the order is free. Only
  S, |x| − |y| with |x| ≥ |y|, needs its operands ordered. The group completion's case therefore
  splits into Knuth's two algorithms, A and S, each with a fixed order: A takes the longer operand
  first for nothing, S the larger.
- **A's size is its final carry's.** A sum of x (m_x digits, normal) and y with m_x ≥ m_y has m_x + c
  digits. With no carry out, digit m_x − 1 is at least x's top digit, which is not 0. So A tracks
  nothing per digit, and only S keeps the scan for its top nonzero digit.
- **The index domain splits** at the shorter size, [0, m_y) ∪ [m_y, m_x), so each piece reads only
  what it has, by its own guard. This is GMP's `mpn_add_n` then `mpn_add_1`, as one loop with two
  arms.

## What was built

- **`addlp` and `sublp`**, each a loop writing through a borrow (as `macrow`), writing its own carry
  slot and header in its exit arm, so nothing reads the buffer after a loop.
- **`addsub`** makes one table of max(m_a, m_b) + 2 entries before a four-way case: add the longer
  first, or subtract the larger first. The choice is in which tables are passed, so no table is
  chosen by an `if`, and R1d's token still reaches the one allocation. Its capacity is the case's
  guard, `render`'s form, so the header's index is a path fact.
- **Each loop clamps its sizes once**, before it, against the lengths it reads and writes. That is
  the identity on every numeral, whose size is below its length by the representation. It makes each
  read's bound a path fact, which `size`'s nested let does not export through the join's templates.

The table is now written through a borrow, so its build is no longer born full: it takes a zeroed
region, a `memset` of about 0.1 ns per digit against the ~2 ns per digit removed.

## What was measured

Every answer equals Go's, and every operation and loop is proven (fib 160 of 160, fact 81 of 81,
D 29,579 of 29,579). Best of five, two rounds, serially and alone:

| program | one A/S loop | A and S split | Go, `math/big` |
|---|---|---|---|
| fib(10⁵) | 198.4 / 203.9 ms | **144.6 / 148.5** | 29.7 / 33.9 |
| 2,000 quotients | 236.6 / 243.0 | **220.0 / 225.0** | 95.2 / 89.3 |
| fact(20,000) | 337.9 / 336.3 | 335.5 / 332.8 | 42.5 / 36.0 |

fib is 27% faster. D gains 7%, its sums being a small part of it, and fact nothing: its product is
`mul_small`, untouched here.

**The cost is code.** Each sum now inlines four loop cases where it inlined one:
- the 2,000-quotient program went from 21,102 operations to 29,579 and took about ten minutes to
  compile, almost all of it in clang's `-O2`;
- in the check, `examples/big/fib` compiled 2.15× slower paired, inside the gate's absolute margin.

This is the inlined-instances item, deliberately not next on the plan, now measured twice. Emitted
as functions, the library's operations would cost their code once and could carry parameter
`noalias` (lenonce-2026-11-10).

**The check**: only `examples/big/`'s `fib` and `pair`, the two that add, change on windows, printing
what Go prints. The counts went from 6,080 of 6,134 operations and 662 of 686 loops to 6,178 of 6,232
and 668 of 692, every added one proven, the unproven 54 and 24 unchanged.
