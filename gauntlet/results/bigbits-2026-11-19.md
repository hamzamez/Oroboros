# bigbits — ADR 0082's operations above the word, 2026-11-19

hamza, 2026-11-19: "Build everything that is decided and wait for a program. You come up with a
program, implement famous programs or algorithms, whatever you think is best for testing and
education." ADR 0082 (2026-10-27) decided the Boolean operations, the shifts and `mod` as ℤ's
2-adic functions on every target, and bits-2026-10-27 built them in the word only: "a bignum
operand or result is refused". This step builds them above it, on two famous programs.

## The programs

- **`examples/big/stein.oro`**: Stein's binary gcd (Stein 1967; Knuth, TAOCP 2 §4.5.2, Algorithm
  B) over Fibonacci numbers, checking Lucas's gcd(F_m, F_n) = F_gcd(m, n). The common power of two
  is the lowest set bit of a | b, `x & -x`. In the 2-adic integers −x = ~x + 1 flips every bit
  above x's lowest 1, so only that bit survives the and. Each odd part is reached by `y >> 1` while
  `y & 1 == 0`, and each subtraction of two odd numbers is followed by one.
- **`examples/big/popcount.oro`**: Kernighan's popcount (Wegner 1960; K&R exercise 2-9).
  `x & (x − 1)` clears the lowest set bit, so the loop runs once per 1. 3^1000 has 758 of its 1585
  bits, and the Mersenne prime 2^4423 − 1 has 4423.
- **`gauntlet/differential/cases/big-bits`**: each of the seven operations past 2^128, with both
  signs. It covers both-negative pairs, floors past the top (`b >> 1000` is −1), words computed
  exactly and brought back (`b & 65535`, `b mod 1000003`), and `(a << 5) & −(a << 5)` = 32. Its 22
  values are Python's, whose integers are 2-adic too.
- **`big-stein`** (7 pairs) and **`big-popcount`** (6 values, among them `m ⊕ t`, two Mersenne
  numbers' xor, the 577 ones between them).

Each case gives one output on Go, JavaScript, the JVM and windows.

## What was built

**The rung (`ir/big.go`).** Supply rule (S) gains ADR 0082's operations:
- an operand held exactly makes the operation exact, and a shift's count stays a word;
- the result is held exactly unless its fact lies in S. There it is brought back by r
  (`big-word`), as `big%-small` does for `%`: `y & 1` ∈ [0, 1], `x mod m` with m a word ∈ [0, m);
- the left shift and `~` are fit to a bounded program's one set (ADR 0029). The others are no
  longer than their operands.

**The host bignums**, one declaration each (`big&`, `big|`, `big⊕`, `big~`, `big<<`, `big>>`,
`big-mod`):
- Go: math/big's And, Or, Xor, Not, Lsh, Rsh and Mod;
- JavaScript: BigInt's `& | ^ ~ << >>`, and `mod` as `r < 0 ? r + m : r` over `%`;
- Java: BigInteger's and, or, xor, not, shiftLeft, shiftRight and mod. A count is clamped to
  2^31 − 1, past which every value BigInteger holds shifts right to 0 or −1.

All three are two's complement on a sign and magnitude, which is the 2-adic reading.

**K(ℕ\*) (`emit/natnum.oro`'s bits).** A value is a sign and a magnitude. For a < 0, a = −A =
~(A − 1), and A − 1 = A′ is the magnitude of a + 1. De Morgan then gives every case from three
digitwise operations on naturals (`lop`: and, and-not with ~v on a digit as dm − v, or, xor):

| | both ≥ 0 | one negative (b) | both negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| a & b | A & B | A & ~B′ | −((A′ \| B′) + 1) |
| a \| b | A \| B | −((B′ & ~A) + 1) | −((A′ & B′) + 1) |
| a ⊕ b | A ⊕ B | −((A ⊕ B′) + 1) | A′ ⊕ B′ |

The other operations reduce the same way:
- ~a = −1 − a;
- a·2ᵏ = `mul_small` by 2^r, then q zero digits inserted, where q = k div d and r = k mod d;
- ⌊A/2ᵏ⌋ is `mul_small` by 2^(d−r), then q + 1 digits dropped, with no division (q digits alone
  when r = 0);
- ⌊−A/2ᵏ⌋ = −((A′ >> k) + 1);
- `mod` is `rem` with m added to a negative remainder.

Each is checked once as a theory and called as a function of the program (`natCalled`).

**Three facts the programs named**, each a refusal before:
1. **guardDiff** (`ir/interval.go`): an arm's π carries its guard x rel o, and relSub meets x − y
   with rel's half-line when o is y. This is the one difference constraint a guard states (Miné
   2001's zones). Euclid's subtractive gcd on words, `p > q -> again(p − q, q)`, was refused on
   every target with p − q ⊤; it is now proven, its claim 0..1000 included.
2. **A let's claim is read end by end** (`emit/refine.go`): `x = fib(m)` bound to `the "int 0
   +inf"` gave nothing, because both ends had to be words. So `odd(x)`'s `0 <= x` failed under a
   `case` arm.
3. **A name bound to a closed integer is that integer**: its value where it is a word, and its
   sign past the word. To the linear layer `a = 3^100` is a ≥ 1, so `b mod a` owes 0 < a and has
   it.

And one more:
- **F13** (`lang-facts.oro`): of two naturals, `|` and `⊕` are naturals, since the 2-adic sign
  bit is acted on like any other. `|` and `⊕` are atoms of the linear layer, as `&` is.

## What failed first

1. **Euclid's subtraction was refused on words** before any bit operation was reached. The
   interval of p − q forgets that the arm compared them. This is the oldest algorithm there is,
   and nothing had asked.
2. **The K(ℕ\*) instances were inlined into `main`** (`natCalled` did not list them): 445,736
   lines of IR in `oro_main` for big-bits' 22 operations. The compile took over ten minutes, and
   the frame passed a page, so LLVM called `__chkstk`, which a program linked without the C
   runtime does not have. Called as functions, the case compiles in 1m43s and links.
3. **The right shift first divided by 2^r** (`qsmall`), and its digits were not proven below B:
   the two-digit lemma needs its guard. The product by 2^(d−r) and one more digit dropped needs no
   division and no lemma.
4. **A shift count's sign was not the instance's.** The library's parameters' sets are not
   assumed, so the count is masked, `k & (d − 1)`, which is in [0, d) by F9 whatever k is. The
   count's `k ≥ 0` is stated as `rem` states its divisor, an `abandon` arm never reached.
5. **F13 was first a shift fact**, `a ≤ a << k`, for the division above. Once the shift stopped
   dividing it was not load-bearing (both programs built without it), and it was removed. The
   facts kept are the ones a program needed.
6. **A contrived arm found an imprecision.** `ones(t − (t − 1))`: the relational difference proves
   x = 1, so the trip bound makes the loop's result fit the word, and no rung is needed. The body's
   `c + 1` is still widened to [1, +∞), and so unproven. The arm was replaced by `m ⊕ t`; the
   imprecision is owed (below).
7. **The closed binder first held the word's end, a ≥ 2^63 − 1.** That overflows the linear
   layer's arithmetic, which then concluded nothing: `mod a` was refused with that very fact
   known. The planted fault's test found it, because its program reads a twice. Read once, β
   substitutes 3^100 and the closed comparison decides the divisor alone, so the fault did not
   bite. The sign is what the layer can hold.
8. **A shift transfer for a count unbounded above** (`1 << n` as [1, +∞)) was built, and was
   withdrawn. Its planted fault left popcount's emitted code byte-identical, because the binder's
   claim demands the value whatever its fact.
9. **`popcount`'s `ones((1 << n) − 1)` is refused, and by design.** A parameter's set is an
   obligation and supplies nothing once inlined (ADR 0079 item 3). The program states its consent
   with a binder's claim, `m ∈ Nat = (1 << n) − 1`.

## Owed

- **A frame above a page does not link on windows**: no `__chkstk` without the C runtime. Calling
  the instances removed this program's case; a function the program writes with a frame that
  large still meets it. It is ADR 0123's question (what the code generator calls is the triple's
  runtime), and an assembly routine is a wall for hamza.
- **A kept definition returning a numeral** is typed as its table at the caller, so `big_str` of
  it refuses it, and the program is inlined whole (big-signed-ops has it too).
- **A loop's counter bounded by its trip count** in the body, not only at its exit (`ones(1)`).
- **The fixed limbs**: none of the seven, refused (`ErrNoLimbForm`).
- **A word mask on a host bignum** (`y & 1` as `y.Bit(0)`): two allocations per step on Go now,
  part of the 0.64×.
- **The termination of Stein's loops**: odd's halving and the main loop's falling maximum are
  measures the size-change check does not see on numerals.
