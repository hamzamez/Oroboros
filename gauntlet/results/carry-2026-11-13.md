# The sum's carry in the flags (the full adder)

Date: 2026-11-13. [ADR 0108](../../docs/decisions/0108-the-full-adder-is-the-triples-and-a-fold-over-a-split-domain-is-two-loops.md).
**`fib(10⁵)` is 1.23× Go's process, from 1.9×** (162 → 108 ms against 88, medians). K(ℕ\*)'s A and S
are two loops in sequence, and each step is the machine's `adc` or `sbb`, chosen by the LLVM printer
where IR_P's types prove the full adder's premise. Every proof count is unchanged (5,710 of 5,764,
649 of 673).

## What was measured first

The recommendation, from the leaf measurements, was the sum's carry: C's `_addcarry_u64` took half the
double word's time per digit (0.98 against 1.98 ns that day). Hand-editing fib's emitted `.ll` to call
`llvm.x86.addcarry.64` instead **gained nothing**: 51 against 51 ms. The assembly showed why. K(ℕ\*)'s
A was one loop whose clauses test i < my at every digit. clang sinks the two clauses' adders into one
body, so each digit costs a compare and a branch, and the carry goes `setb`, `addb $-1`, `adc`, `setb`
around them.

So the C model was redone in the library's shape (scratchpad `div/b.c`, the same 5.4·10⁷ steps, three
runs across the machine's speed states, ns per digit):

| A | double word | the machine's carry | `__builtin_addcll` / `uadd.with.overflow` |
|---|---|---|---|
| one loop, a clause on i < my | 0.67–0.71 | 0.66–0.71 | |
| two loops in sequence | 0.66–0.71 | **0.33–0.35** | 0.88 |

Each change alone gains nothing, and only both together halve the step. LLVM's target-independent
pair is slower than the double word, since LLVM 23 does not combine it into `adc`. So the realization
is the triple's own operation.

## The algebra

- **The fold over a split domain.** [0, mx) = [0, my) ⊔ [my, mx), and A is a fold whose state is the
  carry. A fold over a concatenation is the composite of the folds, the image of ++ under the monoid
  homomorphism (List, ++) → (End(S), ∘). So A is two loops, the first's final carry the second's
  initial one.
- **The full adder.** φ : t ↦ (t mod B, ⌊t/B⌋) is a bijection [0, 2B) → [0, B) × {0, 1}, and so is
  t ↦ (t mod B, [t < 0]) from [−B, B). adc(c, x, y) = φ(x + y + c) and sbb(b, x, y) = φ(x − y − b)
  exactly when x, y < B and c, b ∈ {0, 1}. The machine reads its carry in as c ≠ 0, so {0, 1} is the
  domain, not a convenience.
- **A read through the low half.** t & m = (t mod B) & m for any m ∈ [0, B), since & is 2-adic and m
  has no bit at 64 or above. So a read by any word mask is the low half's, and B − 1 itself, which is
  no literal in IR_P, need not be recognized.

## What was built

- `emit/natnum.oro`: A (`addlp`) and S (`sublp`) are each two loops. The second (`addhi`, `subhi`) is
  a call in the first's exit arm, borrowing the buffer, with the carry, or the borrow and the size so
  far, as arguments.
- `targets/windows/x64.oro`: `add_carry` and `sub_borrow`, `llvm.x86.addcarry.64` and `subborrow.64`,
  beside `divq`.
- `ir/llvm/carry.go`: the printer finds each full adder from IR_P alone:
  - a + or − of the double word, its inner ones used by it alone;
  - its leaves two words and one bit by their types;
  - its uses `& m`, `>> 64` or `< 0`.

  It prints the adder at the top statement, nothing at an inner one, and a projection at each read.
  K(ℕ\*)'s sum and difference print `adc` and `sbb` in both loops, and so does D's multiply-subtract.
- llvm.md §3d; the witnesses `cmd/build/adder_test.go`:
  - **a carry in [0, 2]**: a program summing (2⁶⁴ − 1) + i + (i mod 3) and reading it by its halves
    stays in the double word and answers 997, ℤ's answer. With `PlantAdderFault`, which takes [0, 2]
    as a bit, the adder is chosen and the answer is wrong;
  - **a read that is no half**, the same sum with a bit carry, read by t >> 63: the double word, ℤ's
    answer. With `PlantAdderReadFault`, which takes any shift as the carry, the adder is chosen and the
    answer is wrong;
  - and not vacuous: `fib(300)`'s emitted module imports `addcarry`.

## What failed first

- **The carry across the buffer.** The first version wrote the first loop's carry at index my, where
  x mod B^my + y = Σ o_i·B^i + c·B^my makes it the partial sum's numeral, and the second loop read it
  back. The decision then read it as a digit, [0, B), the table's element type, so the carry's {0, 1}
  was lost and the second loop's sum left the double word. A mask would have been a clamp. The carry
  crosses as an argument instead.
- **A loop yielding a value, then a call with the buffer**: refused, since a buffer a loop yields
  beside a value is frozen (ADR 0031), and so is one a call hands back beside a value. The second loop
  is called in the first's exit arm, the composite in tail position, as `mul`'s exit arm stores.
- **A set on the carry parameter**, `c0 ∈ 0..1`: the refinement layer does not reason about `>>`, so
  the call's obligation was refused. Without it the instance is inlined (ADR 0102), and the IR's
  decision proves the carry in [0, 1].
- **A π of the difference**, made where t < 0 branches, was taken as a use the adder does not read. A
  π is its source under the plan's aliasing, and its reads are counted under the source.
- **The read witness, first version**: t >> 63 is the carry plus 1 on that program, so 1,000 steps
  added exactly 1,000, which the sum mod 1,000 hid. Taken mod 997.

## Measured

Serial, interleaved, 21 rounds, each process timed whole from Python; medians, minima in brackets, ms;
the baseline built at HEAD (`807c6eb`) in a worktree, every output equal to the baseline's and Go's:

| program | before | **the full adder** | Go `math/big` | ratio to Go |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fib(10⁵) | 162 (154) | **108 (101)** | 88 (82) | 1.9 → **1.23** |
| the product, `factm` | 90 (84) | 90 (83) | 95 (89) | 0.94 |
| fact(20,000), printed | 272 (260) | 275 (263) | 102 (99) | 2.7 |
| 2,000 quotients | 244 (225) | 233 (208) | 269 (245) | 0.87 |

- **fib** takes 0.67× its time. Without its printing (the sums alone) it is 93 ms, and a process
  printing one digit is 15 ms. So about 78 of the 108 are the sums and 15 are printing.
- The product, `fact` and the quotients are inside the noise. `fact`'s gap is printing.
- The machine ran about twice as slow today as in class-2026-11-12's sweep (Go's fib 88 against 67).
  The ratios are comparable and the absolute times are not.

## What it cost

- **The compile.** `cmd/check`'s gate flagged two windows compiles, both refused before and after by
  `gen`'s W5 on `nat.add`'s argument (an outcome unchanged, never a binary): `examples/big/fib.oro`
  4.27× paired (234 → 1,000 ms) and `pair.oro` 2.71×. Accepted as slower, logged in ACCEPTED.md.
  Timed serially with `gen` on the measured programs:
  - fib 1.7–3.4 s → 3.6–4.4 s, about 2×;
  - the 2,000 quotients 31.4 → 33.4–35.0 s, 1.09×;
  - `fact` unchanged.

  The likely cause is the second loop's place, inside the first loop's exit arm. Each of the
  decision's passes over the first loop takes the second along, though it runs once, after.
  Analysing an exit arm once, outside its loop's fixpoint, would recover it. That is a change to the
  decision, so it waits ("optimizing the compile" is deliberately not next).
- **No compared text holds the change.** None of the 83 windows files the emission step compares
  contains a K(ℕ\*) function or an i128 (`grep`): a differential case's `run` is not exported, and
  the `examples/big/` programs are refused by `gen` on windows. So the accept changed no file. Since
  ADR 0103, the double word's and now the adder's emitted text has been checked only by behavior: the
  differential's windows runs of every `big-*` case, and the witnesses here. That is a path no text
  comparison runs.

## Printing's leaf, measured for the next step

Measured the same day, in C (scratchpad `div/d.c`), toward `fact`'s gap. A chain of 8.6·10⁶ 2-by-1
divisions by 10¹⁹, each step's remainder the next one's high word (quadratic chunking's inner loop):

| leaf | ns per step |
|---|---|
| `divq` | 12.1 |
| Möller and Granlund's reciprocal (2011, Algorithm 4) | 12.0 |
| a multiplication row of the same count, t = u·c + k | 1.37 |

Both divisions are bound by the remainder's latency, so no leaf rewrite cheapens D. Printing by
multiplications, the fraction method (TAOCP 2 §4.4), costs about 3.5n² multiplication steps, the
reciprocal's computation included, against chunking's 0.53n² division steps. That is about 1.3×
better while multiplication is schoolbook. Go prints 20,000! in about 8 ms by divide and conquer over
Karatsuba, so the gap is asymptotic: Karatsuba first, then printing by a remainder tree.

## What was not built

- **Index-set splitting in the compiler**: only K(ℕ\*) writes a fold whose clauses split its domain,
  and it writes the split itself.
- **The full adder in the product's row** (`macrow`, `mul_small`): t = u·v + w + k is not φ of a sum
  of words, and clang already prints its i128 as `mulq`, `add`, `adc` (nat64-2026-11-12).
- **An unrolled chain**: C's ×4 unrolling measured the same as clang's own (0.34 against 0.33).
- **i686's adder**: the second triple has no `addcarry.64`. Its `addcarry.32` comes with 6b′ step 4.
