# D5: a scalar proven in S₃₂ held in an i32 on windows32

2026-11-17. Item 4 of [assessment-2026-11-17](../../docs/assessment-2026-11-17.md) §5, ADR 0122's
D5, [ADR 0127](../../docs/decisions/0127-a-scalar-in-s32-is-held-in-an-i32-where-the-triple-prefers-it.md).

**On i686, a value the decision proves in S₃₂ is now an i32, and its arithmetic is printed at 32 bits
wherever that is exact.** The research's loop runs at 0.59× its time before, the speed of the same
IR narrowed by hand. Every windows32 program measured is faster, with identical output. x86-64 was
measured too, and does not take it.

## 1. Measured first

The research's loop (`if (i·3 + s) % 7 == 0 { s ← (s + i) % 1000003 }`, 3·10⁸ steps, n from the
argument count so staging cannot fold it), as compiled, the same IR rewritten at i32 by hand, and C.
Five runs, medians:

| | i686 | x86-64 |
|---|---|---|
| ours, the word | 805 ms | 476 ms |
| ours at i32, by hand | 474 ms | 302 ms |
| C `int` | 332 ms | 336 ms |
| C `long long` | 872 ms | 328 ms |

- **On i686 the pairs cost 1.70×.** The remaining 1.43× against C's `int` is the same residue the
  research measured on x86-64 (1.42×, where C's two widths cost the same). So it is neither the
  triple's nor D5's.
- **On x86-64, i32 is 1.58× too**, and beats C. The cause is not the registers, since C's widths
  agree: it is LLVM's `urem` by a constant at 32 bits.

## 2. The algebra

- **The representation.** S₃₂'s is (i32, the signed reading). ρ₃₂ is a function of a value's proven
  range, which IR_P's text already carries for the printer's flags. So nothing is added to the IR.
- **The theorem.** r: ℤ → ℤ/2³² is a ring homomorphism, injective on S₃₂, so an operation on S₃₂
  operands with a result proven in S₃₂ is exact at 32 bits. The exceptions are LLVM's undefined
  behaviour at 32 bits, and they become the rule's conditions: (−2³¹, −1) for `sdiv`/`srem`, and a
  shift count past 31.
- **The conversions** are d and its inverse, `sext` and `trunc`, each the identity on ℤ. A `trunc`
  must be exact by a range, the value's own or its join's.

## 3. What was built

- **`repr scalar 32`**, the member's datum (`emit/target.go`), declared by `targets/windows32`.
- **`ir/llvm/scalar.go`**:
  - ρ₃₂ (`s32Type`, `s32`);
  - the conversion `as`/`asTo`, with its exactness check;
  - the wide scope `wide`;
  - the operation rule `narrow`;
  - three planted faults.
- **The printer's flows** (`ir/llvm/llvm.go`, `carry.go`):
  - the joins (`send`, a loop's initial values, `select`);
  - `ret` and the entry narrowing;
  - a table's index, load, store and length;
  - `tabulate`'s counter, compared at the word when its bound may be 2³¹;
  - the cells, the C calls and the double word, each in a wide scope;
  - the full adder's values, which are absorbed sums' operands and outside its statement.
- **Tests**:
  - `TestAScalarIsHeldWhereItsRangeIs` (ir/llvm): the loop's text is i32, a shift by up to 40 stays
    at the word beside one by up to 20 at i32, and windows' text is unchanged. `PlantNoScalar` and
    `PlantScalarNoDomain` are each seen.
  - `TestAScalarIsHeldWhereItsRangeIsRun` (cmd/build): a program holding 3·10⁹ and doing six
    operations prints on windows32 what it prints on windows. With `PlantScalarWide` (naturals below
    2³² taken for S₃₂) it no longer does: it traps where LLVM reads the poison of an `nsw` that no
    longer holds.

## 4. What failed first

- **A truncation the value's own type did not justify.** UTF-8 validation's loop passes a constant
  typed `int`, the word, into a variable whose range is S₃₂'s. The exactness check refused it, as
  it is meant to. The truncation is exact by the destination's range, which the decision proves of
  the value on that edge, so a join converts by `asTo`.
- **`tabulate`'s bound**: its counter is in [0, n − 1] ⊆ S₃₂, but n may be 2³¹, and a truncated 2³¹
  is −2³¹, which would end the loop at once. It is compared at the word when n is not S₃₂.
- **The full adder** reads values outside its own statement, which its wide scope does not cover.
  Each is now converted where it is read.
- **The inventory** (`TestTheInventoryIsTheWordsTheCompilerKnows`): `scalar` is a word the target
  reader now accepts, and `docs/spec/inventory.md` must list it. The first full check failed there.
- **A stale `gen.exe`** answered "`runtime` begins no form" while counting. The benchmark's first
  `go build` had written to `/tmp`, which Git Bash maps away from native tools (CLAUDE.md's rule).

## 5. Measured

- **windows32, the same compiler with and without `repr scalar 32`**, nine interleaved rounds,
  medians, outputs identical:

  | program | without | with | ratio |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 20000!, product | 91.7 ms | 78.0 ms | 0.850 |
  | 20000!, computed and printed | 176.2 ms | 134.9 ms | 0.766 |
  | fib(200000) mod 10⁹ + 7 | 449.6 ms | 352.7 ms | 0.785 |
  | pair(24) | 14.7 ms | 13.0 ms | 0.883, near the process-start floor |
  | the sieve (`examples/table/sieve-win`) | 40.5 ms | 29.7 ms | 0.734 |
  | the loop | 807.0 ms | 477.5 ms | 0.592 |

  The loop as printed runs in 472 ms, the same as the hand-narrowed IR.
- **The differential suite on windows32**: 72 of 74 as before. The two left are Go-only
  (`u128-fact`, `u128-ops`). Run before and after the runtime's regeneration.
- **The corpus on windows32**: of 105 sources, 103 emit, and all 103 assemble with clang for i686.
  14,983 operations are at i32 and 14,181 at i64, with 12,330 `sext` and 9,474 `trunc`, which LLVM
  folds where a value meets its own width.
- **x86-64, the same A/B**:
  - big integers neutral: 20000! 1.002 and 1.017, fib 0.994, pair 0.977;
  - the sieve 0.868 and the loop 0.658;
  - but the gauntlet (two harnesses built from the files regenerated each way, all 22 cases agreeing
    with Go's answers on both, five rounds): the tokenizer 1.040 and the tree 1.060, smooth 0.997
    and 1.011, the hand-written controls 0.998 to 1.003.

  Not declared on x86-64 (ADR 0127 item 5). Only three gauntlet files change there at all: the
  others' indices are not in S₃₂ under a 2⁴⁷-element address space.

## Paid and bought

- **Compile time**: 1.00× the baseline by the check's gate (the median of 64 compiles over 300 ms),
  whose targets do not declare the scalar. windows32's compile is not gated (not in `cmd/check`); its
  cost is a range test per value and per operation.
- **Run time**:
  - windows32 is 0.59× to 0.88× on every program measured, and slower on none;
  - x86-64 is unchanged (not declared).
- **Code size**: windows32's text grows by the conversions, which are folded at -O2. Not measured as
  bytes. Owed.
- **Proofs and refusals**: unchanged. The rule reads proofs and makes none.
- **Compiler lines**:
  - `ir/llvm/scalar.go` +235 (of which about 70 lines are its comment);
  - `ir/llvm/llvm.go` +84 −23;
  - `ir/llvm/carry.go` +6 −4;
  - `emit/target.go` +17 −1.
- **Tests**: +137 (`ir/llvm/scalar_test.go` 85, `cmd/build/scalar_test.go` 52).
- **Target lines**: `targets/windows32/windows32.oro` +5.
- **Emitted text changed**:
  - windows32's almost everywhere, and its runtime `lib/rt/i686.ll` +19 −16;
  - every other target byte-identical (the emission step).
- **Rules made less general**: none.

## Not built, and owed

- **U₃₂**: a digit below 2³² in an i32 read unsigned, for K(ℕ\*) on i686, the measured remainder of
  windows32's big-integer gap.
- **A rule by use on x86-64**: narrow a value only where it is never an address, measured against
  the gauntlet.
- **The loop's 1.43× against C's `int`**, on both triples: not the width. Owed to the standing
  measure.
- **windows32's emission and its compile time in `cmd/check`**: hamza's.
- **windows32's code size**.
