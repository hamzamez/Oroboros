# decimals-2026-11-01: decimals, the rounding and ℤ ≤ N (ADR 0086, numbers.md §9 part 3)

Part 3 of step 1 of [ADR 0086](../../docs/decisions/0086-a-number-is-an-element-of-z-tenth-and-a-rounding-is-its-one-lossy-operation.md):
the first values of N that are not doubles. Program first: `examples/num/ledger.oro`, the subtotal,
8.25% tax and total of a file of `PRICE QUANTITY` lines, equal to a hand-written Go program on Go, the
JVM and JavaScript.

## What was built

**The surface (J, D, the reducer):**
- `round(S, e)` and `round(S, e, 'mode)`, with modes `'nearest` (ties to even, the default),
  `'half_away`, `'down`, `'up`, `'zero`, `'away` and `'sat`. D carries the set as its canonical type and
  the mode as its name: `(round "dec 2 0 1000000000" e "half-away")`.
- `÷`, ASCII `\div`, at `/`'s level.
- `D(k, lo..hi)`, a decimal grid, "dec K LO HI" canonically, shown `D(k, lo..hi)` in messages.
- `round` and `÷` are in Σ_L, pure, and no target declares them.

**The order-sorted signature ℤ ≤ N (numbers.md §1):**
- The checker's rules:
  - N's `+ − ·`, order and equality apply when an operand is N's or a numeral. An integer goes where N is
    wanted, and a conditional of N and ℤ is N.
  - A rounding's sort is ℤ's onto a set of integers, N's onto any other.
  - A numeral is still typed as the double it was, and the double and N agree both ways in the
    checker's `agree` (not in `compatible`, which W5 reads). Only the IR's typing tells a numeral from a
    host's double, and it refuses a real mix.
  - N's rule walks an operation's operands twice only when a cheap test, cached and linear, says an
    operand may be N's.
- The IR's unifier has two absorbing literal sorts:
  - `#int`, an integer literal, becomes `int` or N;
  - `#dec`, a numeral, becomes the host's double or N.

  Each defaults to what it was before N existed, and meets anything else as it did. This is Go's
  untyped constants, and Haskell's defaulting (Report §4.3.4).

**The scaled word (`ir/numbers.go`, numbers.md §6.1):**
- A value of N gets a static scale k by the least fixed point of the filtration's two laws:
  - Nₐ + N_b ⊆ N_max(a,b);
  - Nₐ·N_b ⊆ N_(a+b).

  It is held as its numerator m = x·10ᵏ, the abstraction function (H, d) of ADR 0083. Every operation of
  N becomes the integer operations that compute it on numerators. A rounding is integer division with a
  mode, from the Euclidean remainder. The decision run after proves every one inside the word.
- At an entry point a decimal is its numerator in the host's word.

**Obligations, each decided or the program refused:**
- **A rounding's value inside its set, by monotonicity.** r is a monotone retraction onto a grid holding
  the set's ends, so u ÷ d inside them puts r(u ÷ d) inside, decided at the corners of u's and d's
  intervals. The expansion's own branches lose this: 2r > d has no interval.
- **A `÷`'s divisor not zero**, on the intervals. A divisor of both signs is refused, since ≠ 0 has no
  interval.
- **x ∈ D(k, lo..hi)** at an internal function's parameter: a call obligation, decided on the IR from the
  argument's scale and its numerator's interval, and never by the refinement layer.
- **A finer value in a coarser declared grid** is refused, saying to round it.
- **A scale that grows around a loop** (y ← y·0.5) is refused past 10⁻³⁰.

**`lib/num.oro`: `num.decimal(x, k)`**, a number's numeral with exactly k places. It is one rounding and
digit arithmetic, written in the language, so it prints the same on every target.

## What was measured

- **The ledger** (`examples/num/ledger.oro`, 94 lines):
  - 255 of 255 integer operations proven and 7 of 7 loops terminating, on all three targets.
  - Its output equals the hand-written Go reference (`experiments/num/ledger`, int64 cents) on Go, the
    JVM and JavaScript. That held for 3,000 random lines (28 KB) and for the edges: a tie in the tax
    ($2.00 × 8.25% = 0.165, so 0.17), an empty file, a third decimal place, a malformed price.
  - JavaScript's numerators fit its ±(2⁵³ − 1) because the program states its limits.
- **The roundings against the specification** (`TestRoundingIsTheSpecifications`): every mode, both
  signs, every tie. Each of the 7,260 quotients a ÷ b of numerators on D(1, −6..6) × D(1, ±(1..3)), and
  4,001 roundings of D(3) onto D(1), interpreted over ℤ on the expanded IR, equal an exact rational
  reference.
- **Speed** was not measured. The ledger reads at most 64 KB, process start dominates, and the decimal
  operations are a few integer operations per line.
- **The corpus** (`go run ./cmd/check -skip tooling`, accepted): every existing program byte-identical.
  The eight changes are the two new sources on four targets: `ledger` and `lib/num`, emitted on Go,
  the JVM and JavaScript, and refused on windows, which has no `ReadFile` or `print_line`. The counts
  are now 4,773 of 4,825 operations and 497 of 520 loops, against 4,008 of 4,060 and 476 of 499, every
  new operation and loop proven. Compile time is 1.00× the baseline. Three `freq` compiles were
  flagged at 1.24–1.26× and cleared by the serial pair.

## What failed first, what it cost

- **A π-parameter on a number stated a relation at the wrong scale.** Lower writes `Of Rel Other`
  between values. After the rewrite, `sum < 10^10` on a sum held in cents compared numerators at scales 2
  and 0, so the π claimed m_sum < 10¹⁰, a hundredth of the truth. Here it only failed to prove; it could
  have proven something false. `realign` (numbers.go) repairs it:
  - a π against a constant at the lower scale gets the constant's numerator at Of's scale, hoisted to
    the function's entry, which dominates every arm;
  - any other π on a number is removed, and its value renamed back to Of: the identity, which narrows
    nothing.
- **The refinement layer reasoned about numbers of N as integers.** Its fragment is linear arithmetic over
  ℤ, so `sum < 10^10` became sum ≤ 9999999999, and `x > 9 && x < 10` a contradiction, false of 9.5.
  - Repair: `emit/numnames.go` names every binder of sort N, joining the checker's own inference with a
    syntactic closure over λ, `let` and loops. A linear fact or goal over one is assumed as nothing and
    proven by nothing (`facts.assumeLE`, `assumeEQ`, `entails`).
  - Test: `TestNoFactAboutANumberIsAssumedInZ`. Planted fault: with the filter off it knew x ≥ 10 and
    x ≤ 9.
  - Cost: the refinement layer proves nothing about numbers. The IR's numerators do instead.
- **A D claim on an internal function's parameter was not enforced at all**: `g(y)` with y up to 10
  passed into x ∈ D(1, 0..5). Repaired as above, and tested both ways and through a guard (`y <= 5`
  proves it, `y <= 6` does not).
- **The ledger's digit loop, x ← 10x + digit while x < 10⁶, was not bounded**: [0, 2⁶³ − 1].
  - The cause was an old limit of the interval analysis, present at HEAD with integers alone. The
    threshold set held 10⁶ but not the guard's image near 10⁷, so widening jumped to the word, and the
    arm that keeps x carried +∞ forever.
  - Repair: Halbwachs, Proy and Roumanoff's widening up to (1997). The guards' constants are closed
    once under the body's constant scalings, c·m. Any threshold set is sound, since the result is
    checked as a post-fixpoint. The constants the pass aligns are folded, so a guard still compares with
    a constant (`sum < 10^10` on cents compares with 10¹²).
  - A function contract with a declared result and a claim binder were tried first and did not bound it.
- **Two refusals in the mark route took every other mark's decision with them.** A rounding obligation
  unproven there, and the checker panicking on a `where` it was not written for. Now the route records
  the pass's obligations and does not decide them (Entry does), and NumNames recovers.
- **The checker had typed a numeral as the host's double**, so `y * 0.5` was refused as integer
  arithmetic, and a `round`'s argument was never checked (an unknown primitive returned "").
  - Typing a numeral as N instead broke six gauntlet programs, `loop acc = 0.0` summed with `go.f+`.
  - Typing it as no sort let a table of numerals be read as a String: `TestATablesReadAndLengthAreTyped`
    caught that.
  - The arrangement kept is the one above.
- **Compile time rose from 1.05× to 1.24× the baseline.**
  - Most of it was NumNames running the checker for every refiner, now skipped for a residual that makes
    no value of N.
  - The checker's double walk of arithmetic is now gated by the cheap test.
  - The emission sweep then read 1.13–1.24× across runs.
- **A heredoc ate `\n` in a Go test**, CLAUDE.md's lesson again, and a backup went to Git Bash's `/tmp`.

## What was not built

- **A value of N held as a double**, F64 as a set, `round(F64, …)`, the scope `round(F64) { … }`, and the
  decode: part 4.
- **A number of N at a host call, in a table, a map or a cell a host writes**: refused, saying so.
- **A rounding onto a set named by the program** (`set Price = D(2, …)` then `round(Price, …)`): its set
  must be written as `D(…)` in the rounding, since D converts a set it can read and the reducer does not
  yet unfold a named one there.
- **A divisor of unknown sign proven nonzero by a `where`**: the refinement layer, which could read
  `b != 0`, says nothing of numbers. A program splits the signs.
- **Q(k, R), F(p, …) and the transcendentals.**
- **The internal sort's renaming**: N is `num`, and the host's double is still `f64`. Part 4 makes F64 a
  set of N with the double its representation.
