# findings — the book's findings 100 and 101 built, 2026-11-20

hamza, 2026-11-20: "put findings 100 and 101 in the plan", then "Build all 5", these first.

Each was a construct its spec states, refused in the compiler's internal words:
- **100**, a map buffer read in its scope (maps.md §3), two shapes: (a) a `case` on the read in the
  scope's tail with arms that print, refused as a closure; (b) a read and then a store, its capacity
  claim not following;
- **101**, a table of tables (tables.md §5.1) or a table of tuples written as a list (products.md),
  read at an index not known when compiling.

## The programs

Each is a famous algorithm whose shape is the finding's, checked against a computation of its own,
on Go, JavaScript, the JVM and both windows triples (`TestTheBooksFindings100And101`):
- **`examples/map/collatz.oro`: stopping times memoized.** L(2k) = L(k) + 1, so for an even n the
  answer is the map's at n/2 plus one: a read of m[n/2], then a write of m[n]. L(27) = **111**, and
  **20** of 20 even ones agree with iterating.
- **`examples/map/twosum.oro`: Two Sum, one pass and a map.** At a[j] the map is asked for t − a[j];
  the arms write the answer's cells, then a[j] is stored. The pair for 9 is (**0, 1**), and **15**
  of 15 targets agree with the quadratic search.
- **`examples/table/magic.oro`: Lo Shu**, a table of tables. **8** of 8 lines sum to 15, and **9**
  entries occur once each.
- **`examples/table/shoelace.oro`: the shoelace formula** over a list of corners. Twice the area is
  **12**, **50**, **−50** (the 3-4-5 triangle, a square, the square clockwise).
- and, inline in the test, a `case` on a read whose arms write a cell, then an insert: **51**.

## What failed first

- **The first rule went in the reducer and could never fire.** Under the scope's binders a read's
  head is an index, `#0.0`, not the name `#vm` that says it is a version, so the reducer cannot
  tell a map read from a function applied. Removed (a path nothing runs). The translation knows
  the name.
- **The lowering's bug was the cause of (a).** `eliminator` took `((X k) K)` for a map read
  whenever X was not a name, so (((#vm 9) L) K) read L as a key, which the closure refusal then
  named.
- **(b) was a version that was the same map.** The threading of a state through arms that do not
  touch it made a new name with no fact.
- **Collatz met two more shapes the moment it was written:** the read under an `if`, where the
  reducer's case-of-case pushed the pattern in (so the translation's rule never saw it), and the
  pattern over the loop in a definition's tail, inlined on Go but kept as a function on windows.
- **Flattening the literal of tuples built projections the flat literal could not read:** the
  arity lives on the tuple literal, so a literal head's projections are uncurried at once.
- **Two Sum's quadratic search hit two reader rules:** an `again` under a guard chain (`case { φ
  -> … }` is `if`, ADR 0015) and after a single-name binding over a nested loop. It is written with
  the condition among the loop's clauses and the inner search as a definition.
- **The first full check caught two regressions of mine**, each a meaning changed silently:
  - the literal of tuples flattened wherever it stood, so `fmt.Println((n, n))`, a variadic's
    table of one pair, became a table of two values and was accepted. Flattening is sound only
    where every use reads through the arity (a pattern on an element, a projection, `len`), so a
    literal is flattened at a read's head, or bound to a name every use of which is one
    (`onlyReads`); handed whole to anything else it keeps its tuples and is refused as before;
  - the lowering's tightened read refused a map that is a named form's value, `(map …)` or
    `build-map(…)`, at the read's head (the book's `lookup`, TestAScalarCellIsTheHostsCell). The
    bad shape was a variable applied, `(#vm k)`; a named form is a map (`isMapTerm`).
- **A flattening of a table of tables into one table and its offsets** (compressed rows) was tried
  first in J. Its segment's formation obligation, off(k) ≤ off(k + 1) ≤ len(flat), is true by
  construction and not provable: it relates two reads of one table, and a fact is a schema over one
  trigger. The n-way choice needs no such fact.
