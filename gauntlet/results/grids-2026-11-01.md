# grids-2026-11-01: one rounding procedure for every grid, checked against the definition

A step between ADR 0086's step 1 and step 2, from hamza's question: floats-research §18.1 defined F64 as
a library set, `F(53, -1022, 1023)`, and part 4 ([doubles-2026-11-01](doubles-2026-11-01.md)) built it
into the compiler. Nothing changed in the ontology: F64 is a set of N and the checker's sorts are
ℤ ≤ N. What the compiler gained is F64's decision procedure and its representation. Part 4 built
binary64's procedure alone, from the host's conversion, and nothing checked it against the definition.

## What was built

**The grid's algebra** (`core/grid.go`). A grid G ⊂ ℚ is closed, discrete and **locally uniform**:
each x lies in a closed cell Ī(x), its ends in G, on which G ∩ Ī = u(x)·ℤ ∩ Ī.
- ℤ: u = 1. 10⁻ᵏℤ: u = 10⁻ᵏ.
- F(p, emin, +∞): the binade [2ᴱ, 2ᴱ⁺¹] with E = max(⌊log₂|x|⌋, emin), and its mirror, with
  u = 2^(E−p+1). Its elements are m·u for 2ᵖ⁻¹ ≤ m ≤ 2ᵖ, the last being the next binade's first, and
  below 2^emin, for 0 ≤ m ≤ 2ᵖ⁻¹, with the same u.

Both of x's neighbours lie in Ī(x), so **r_G,μ(x) = u·r_ℤ,μ(x/u)** for every mode, a tie to even
being m even in the cell's generator, numbers.md §3's canonical one. One procedure serves every grid:
F64 is F(53, −1022, 1023), and F32, BF16 and the FP8 formats are the same code with other parameters.
The set F(p, emin, emax) is the grid within ±(2 − 2^(1−p))·2^emax. `ir/doubles.go` now folds constants,
decides membership, and takes ⌊q⌋ and ⌈q⌉ by this procedure, where it used the host's conversion, and
`ir/numbers.go`'s integer rounding is its r_ℤ.

**A normal form with one spelling** (`core.SeparatedGrid`). Part 4's normal form of a separation of F64
kept the bounds as written, so `{x ∈ F64 : x <= 1e100}` and `{x ∈ F64 : x <= ⌊1e100⌋}`, one set, had
two canonical types. By the Galois connection of G ↪ ℚ, **G ∩ [a, b] = G ∩ [⌈a⌉_G, ⌊b⌋_G]**, and the
reader now writes those ends. A strict bound is the adjunction once more: x < b is x ≤ the greatest
element below b, so `0 < x && x < 1` is [2⁻¹⁰⁷⁴, 1 − 2⁻⁵³]. A separation's base may be F64 or
F64 ∩ [lo, hi]. A big integer literal as a bound is read now too, where only an int64 one was.

## What was measured

- **`TestTheGridIsItsDefinition`**: F(3, −2, 2) is enumerated from floats-research's definition,
  { m·2^(e−p+1) : m ∈ −(2ᵖ−1)..2ᵖ−1, e ∈ emin..emax + 1 }, each element's canonical m being its m at
  the least e. Every mode (down, up, zero, away, nearest, half-away) and membership are checked at
  every quarter of the finest spacing over ±2^(emax+1), 1,025 points, and at five rationals no grid
  holds, and the procedure equals the definition at all of them. Planted: the cell's exponent one
  binade too high, which the test sees.
- **`TestF64IsBinary64`**: the p = 53 instance against Go's correctly rounded binary64, nearest, floor
  and ceiling, at ties near 1, below the normal range and at its bottom, at the largest finite value,
  past the overflow tie, at 1/10, 1/3 and 2/3, and at 2,000 random rationals up to 200 bits a side. The
  procedure, written from the cell's algebra, and the host agree everywhere.
- **`TestASeparationOfAGridHasOneNormalForm`**: two spellings of one set read as one type, a strict
  bound included, and a separation by any other formula keeps its `where`.
- **The corpus** (`cmd/check`): every program's emission, outcome and proof counts byte-identical to the baseline (4,801 of 4,853 operations, 502 of 525 loops), since every double the procedure computes is the one the host's conversion gave; compile time 0.97×.

## What failed first, what it cost

- **ℤ's normal form found a soundness hole, older than this work.** A separation of ℤ by bounds is
  the range ⌈a⌉..⌊b⌋, and normalizing it changed `TestASeparationIsItsSetAndItsFormula`: a result
  `(r ∈ set { z ∈ Int : z >= 10 })` became `(r ∈ 10..)`, and the program returning 3 was accepted.
  At HEAD, without this work, **`fn h(y ∈ -5..5) (Nat) { y }` is accepted and returns −5**.
  - The cause: `core/read.go` makes a result's range an `ensure` only when its ends fit the word, and
    treats a range with an infinite end as "a REPRESENTATION declaration and not a contract". The
    reason given is that the interval domain reports a bignum as [−∞, +∞], so the claim could never be
    discharged.
  - `CheckEnsures` then skips a result above the word as "enforced at run time". But ADR 0029 item 2
    says a range with an infinite end denotes ℤ, "enforced nowhere", and a result held in the word is
    never a bignum to enforce on.
  - So `Nat` and `10..` on a result are decided by nothing, against ADR 0072 and ADR 0079 (a declared
    set is a claim). Both premises predate ADR 0079.
  - Repairing it needs the interval domain to carry at least a bignum's sign, or the four `Nat`
    programs of `examples/big` would be refused. That is ADR 0029 and 0079's design, brought to hamza
    and not built here. Until then ℤ's separations stay separations, a `where` and an `ensure`, which
    are decided, so the normal form never makes a decided claim undecided.
- **The checker's sort for a rounding onto F64 stays the double's.** Typing it N, with F64 a set
  decided by the IR (`numSortsAgree`, beside `intSortsAgree`), broke report and smooth. A value of N
  then meets a host's `go.Value` and `Array(N)` meets `Array(F64)`. The checker needs N's
  representation-bearing subsets at every boundary, as ADR 0033 gave ℤ's, and that is its own step.
  The change is reverted.
- **The test's own spellings were wrong twice.** I wrote the digits of ⌊10⁷⁹⌋ for ⌊10¹⁰⁰⌋_F64, and
  `5e-324`, Go's shortest rendering of 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁴, which as an exact numeral lies above it. The procedure
  was right both times.

## What was not built

- **The definitions in `lib/num.oro`**: F(p, …) is a replacement set (ADR 0061, not built), and its
  separation form needs ∃ or a logarithm. The procedure and its test stand in for them.
- **F(p, …) as a set a program names**: the procedure takes any p, but J has no former for it, and no
  target realizes a rounding onto F32 or BF16. Its trigger is a program that needs one.
- **ℤ's normal form**, waiting on the hole above; **D(k, R)'s**, whose canonical form has integer
  ends where ⌈a⌉ in 10⁻ᵏℤ need not be one.
- **The checker's N at its boundaries**, above.
