# An integer constant is an element of ℤ

Date: 2026-11-22. hamza, 2026-11-22: "do 1 and 2 now. I would suggest even more, since Integers are
Z, why not fold them at compiler time? … what does the algebra say" (ADR 0156), on chapter 1 §1.3
and the book's finding 120.

## The law

A literal denotes an element of ℤ, the initial ring, and its constants are the ring's nullary
operations. A fold of a pure operation on constants is an equation of ℤ (ADR 0114): 2⁶² · 4 = 2⁶⁴
holds as 7 · 7 = 49 does, so folding it at any size changes no answer (ADR 0009). The old limit,
"inside the word", was ADR 0009's when an `Int` was the word; since ADR 0026, 0147 and 0150 every
target computes ℤ, and the limit was the reducer's carrier, an `int64`, taken for the language's set.

A constant's representation is ρ of its point: the word where its point is inside, otherwise held
exactly. A point is computed by nothing, so no representation recombines it; and a constant outside
S is supply (integers.md §1a), which the code had not built for a constant.

## What was built

1. **The exact fold** (core/reduce.go, `foldExact`): `+ − · / %` on closed integers, a literal or a
   numeral, at every size; the quotient truncated and the remainder the dividend's sign; a division
   by zero not folded. Inside the word it is `foldInt`, unchanged. `PlantFoldInWord`.
2. **The numeral in normal form** (`Numeral`): a value past `int64` is the reader's base-10¹⁵
   numeral with each application folded where the word holds it, the form reduction always left a
   literal in, so every subterm is again normal and the windows32 runtime's 2⁶³ is the text it was;
   `ShowTerm` shows it as the decimal.
3. **A point is its constant** (ir/double.go): `RecombineYields` takes no yield whose fact is a
   point. `PlantRecombinePoints`. Finding 120's cause: the literal's sum lies in E, so its yield
   into ℤ was recombined from its digits as if E computed it, and the rung then held the sum in ℤ,
   leaving the recombination's word operations over it unbounded.
4. **A constant outside S is supply** (ir/exact.go, ir/big.go): D_A demands it and the rung holds it
   exactly, written as its spine, where a call or the function's result reads it; where only the
   pair's operations, a join or a table's storage read it, it stays as it was, the pair's two digits
   (ADR 0151). `PlantConstantInTheWord`.
5. **Tests**: `TestAFoldIsExactInTheIntegers` and `TestFoldingOnAFullWordIsExact` (core, every
   formerly unfolded row now stating its exact integer); `TestAPointIsItsConstant` (cmd/build, three
   constants printed on four targets) and `TestAPointIsItsConstantAtAnExport` (cmd/gen, the exports
   on four targets, its plant refusing Go); `TestAConstantOutsideTheWordIsHeldExactly` (JavaScript).
   `TestAQuotientByANumeralIsAlgorithmDs`' operands made opaque (a loop's 0), since the reducer now
   divides literals itself and Algorithm D would not run.
6. **The book**: chapter 1 §1.3 (`too_big` is 18446744073709551616, `7 / 0` refused when compiled);
   chapter 0 §0.5 (10²⁰ computed while compiling, written from its digits).

## What failed first

- **The supply rule for every constant** broke `pairs.oro` on JavaScript: a constant of U joined with
  pair values became a BigInt, and the join had no common representation. Constants follow ADR 0151:
  the pair where the pair reads them.
- **The pair for every constant of U** left the wrong answer: a constant read by a print stays a
  word-typed Number, since no pair operation makes its digits. A call's argument and the result are
  what neither the pair nor a table's storage holds, and only those take the BigInt.
- **A table literal is no sink** for a constant of S₆₄ on JavaScript (its storage is 64-bit): the
  first use test refused the one-set test's `array { 9223372036854775807 }`.
- **The numeral as the reader writes it** made the windows32 runtime's 2⁶³ a multiplication again,
  where reduction had left it folded; the normal form restored the text.
