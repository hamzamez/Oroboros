# P3: a sparse interval analysis on C2 with π-parameters

2026-09-25. The third prototype of [docs/ir-research.md](../../docs/ir-research.md) §11, done before
P2 because it tests the largest claim. P1 ([irp1-2026-09-25](irp1-2026-09-25.md)) found that 57.7% of
`freq`'s compile allocation is the interval pass copying its environment at branches, because facts
are path-sensitive, and that only a representation in which **each name carries one fact** removes it
(research §4b: e-SSA/SSI π-nodes, sparse analysis).

Code:
- `experiments/irproto/ir.go`: C2 lowering with **π-parameters**. A guard renames each variable it
  tests on each side of the branch by overriding that binder's frame entry. A guard on `(len X)`
  renames X and narrows its length, and a connective contributes the guards it implies.
- `experiments/irproto/sparse.go`: **the sparse analysis.** One fact per value in a dense vector: an
  interval, and for a table a length and an element range (smashing).
  - A π is narrowed once, at its definition.
  - A loop is widened after three rounds, then descends two rounds, then is evaluated once more, and
    only that last evaluation counts.
  - Nothing is ever copied.
- `emit/classify.go`: `ArithOp` and `CmpOp`, thin exports of this package's own classification, so
  the prototype counts and narrows exactly as `interval.go` does. Both count the same operations on
  every program: 218 on `freq`, 515 on `tree`, and so on.

## 2. Precision: a floor, and what is missing is named

| program | `interval.go` | sparse |
|---|---:|---:|
| `io/freq` | 218 of 218 | 138 of 218 |
| `json/tree` | 515 of 515 | 373 of 515 |
| `json/tokenize` | 145 of 145 | 90 of 145 |
| `io/jsonfmt` | 95 of 95 | 69 of 95 |
| `table/sieve` | 8 of 8 | 2 of 8 |
| `big/limbs` | 5 of 5 | 3 of 5 |
| `io/wc` | 2 of 2 | 2 of 2 |

This is expected: the prototype is intervals, lengths and element ranges, and nothing more. What it
leaves unproven, read on `jsonfmt`, is the specialised **relational** theorems `interval.go` carries.
None of them depends on the representation:
- **monotone loops** (`monotone.go`). A scanner returns at least its start. Without it, the exit
  `(< j 0) j` makes the caller's index unbounded below, and every `(+ i 1)` after it is unproven;
- **accumulation over a trip count** (`interval.go`'s bounded increments). `n` grows by a bounded
  amount per trip over at most `len src` trips;
- **`x ≤ x·x`** (`narrowSquare`), which is the sieve's `i·i < n`.

Research §5 places each in the relational factor of the reduced product (linear facts), where each
becomes a fact about *values*. A port adds those domains. It does not change the representation that
made this cost what it is.

**Two gaps the first run exposed were the prototype's, and are fixed:** a guard on `(len src)`, whose
fact belongs to `src`'s length; and a host call's table result, which has a declared element type.
`jsonfmt` went from 63 to 69.

## 3. Soundness, checked the only way a prototype can be

- **On every program, the sparse count is at most `interval.go`'s.**
- **On the programs that exist to be refused** (`examples/int/`), it proves nothing `interval.go`
  leaves unproven:
  - `collatz` 0 of 4 against 0 of 4;
  - `power` 0 of 3 against 0 of 3;
  - `fib` 1 of 2 against 1 of 2;
  - `digit-sum` 0 of 1 against 1 of 1;
  - `sum-range` 1 of 2 against 2 of 2.

  That is consistent with soundness and is not a proof of it. The port's per-operation
  planted-fault table is the proof (research §5).

## 4. Verdict

- **The cost claim holds, by one to two orders of magnitude.** Removing the path-sensitive
  environment (π-parameters) and the rebuilt terms (unique names) takes `freq`'s interval analysis
  from 1.94 s / 2.04 GB to 70 ms / 56 MB, lowering included.
- **Precision is a porting question, not a representation question.** P3's criterion was "kill the
  one-engine plan if the counts differ", and a floor cannot meet it as stated. The counts differ by
  exactly the theorems not yet ported. So the gate moves to the port: **per-program proof counts that
  never fall**, theorem by theorem.
- **C2's iteration is its syntax.** The analysis is a recursion over regions, Bourdoncle's recursive
  strategy with no ordering computed. That is evidence for C2 over C1 on the analysis side. P2 decides
  the printing side.

## 5. Found on the way

- **Nested fixpoints dominate what is left.** `freq`'s 1,464 operations are evaluated 638,952 times,
  because an inner loop reruns its own fixpoint on every round of the outer one. An inner loop is a
  *function of its inputs' facts*, so its result can be memoised per input vector. That is cheap in
  the sparse form, where the vector is the key. It is the next win, and not built.
- **56 MB on `freq` is still mostly avoidable.** Fact slices are allocated per terminator and per
  round (`facts`, `joinAll`), and preallocated per loop they would not be.

## 6. Not built

- The relational domains (§2), content facts, the u64 and bignum representation passes, termination:
  the port.
- Soundness per (domain, operation): the port's planted-fault table.
- P2, the Go printer at parity on the gauntlet.
