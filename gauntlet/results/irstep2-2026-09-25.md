# IR step 2: the Go backend is the IR's printer

2026-09-25. ADR 0032's second step. `gen` and `build` now print Go from **IR_P** through
`ir/golang`, a model of Σ in Go (docs/spec/ir.md §9), instead of from terms through
`emit/golang.go`. The term backend stays reachable as `-printer terms`, for comparison.

## 1. What was built

| file | lines of code | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| `ir/golang/golang.go` | 1,024 | the printer: Σ spelled in Go, with the measured rules of spec §9.4 |
| `ir/final.go` | 404 | the step IR_A → IR_P: Theorem D′, the reduced product, erasing `the` |
| `ir/interval.go` | 632 | the interval domain on the IR (P3's, ported to Σ, with its two holes closed) |
| `ir/restrict.go` | 250 | bounds-check re-slicing as a rewrite by law L13, under its premise |

It replaces 1,706 lines (`golang.go` and `golang_join.go`). Those also lean on `emit/`'s shared
inference (`typeOf`, `elemTypeFixed`, `LoopOneJoin`, `openFresh`), which the printer does not use,
because it reads types.

**Two operations were added to Σ**, each with its law (spec §1.2, §1.4):
- **`assume c`**, the dual of `require`: `if c then skip else ⊥`. An export's `where` is lowered to
  one at entry, because at an export the precondition is assumed (ADR 0028). The interval domain
  reads it with Theorem E's reasoning.
- **`restrict t n`**, the restriction of a table to Fin n, whose obligation is n ≤ len t. Law **L13**:
  `index (restrict t n) i = index t i` for i < n ≤ len t.

## 2. The rules, stated on the IR

| rule | as built | premise |
|---|---|---|
| soleExit | a loop result every break passes as parameter p is p | none |
| PostVars | a parameter every continue advances by one literal goes to the post clause | the value is read nowhere else |
| connectives | `(if c true E)` prints `c \|\| E`, `(if c E false)` prints `c && E` | E is an expression tree |
| element widths | ρ_T of the IR_P type (Theorem D′) | the reduced product (§3) |
| bounds-check re-slicing | `restrict Y (len X)` before the loop, printed `Y[:n]` | **len X ≤ len Y by an `assume`** |
| buffer reuse | a spare alternates with the back-edge build | Rule R's four conditions |
| checked arithmetic | mode `trap` prints the target's `Checked` form | the mode, decided by legality |

**The re-slice has its premise now.** Today's term backend re-slices with none, and the spec §9.4
witness panics on a legal program. `TestRestrictNeedsItsPremise` fails when the premise check is
removed, which is the bug that shipped. `TestNoRestrictWithoutItsPremise` prints the witness and
finds no re-slice. dot's re-slice is discharged by its `where`, len p = len q.

## 3. Element widths: a reduced product, and what measurement said

A table class's representation is fixed by any declaration it touches (Theorem D′). Otherwise it is
ρ_T of the **meet of two sound factors**:
- **the term factor**: `BufferRange` on each build's own λ, written at lowering, plus literal hulls;
- **the IR factor**: flow-insensitive array smashing, the join of every value ever placed in the
  class, with each value's fact from the IR's interval domain.

The meet is sound because each factor is: γ(a ⊓ b) ⊇ γ(a) ∩ γ(b).

**What the first version got wrong, in order:**
1. Threading the element fact through the loop fixpoint with weak updates cannot recover from
   widening. Once el is ⊤, `el(set t i x) = el(t) ⊔ x` keeps it ⊤. Smashing is flow-insensitive in
   the literature, and making it so recovered `stk`.
2. The IR domain ignored `where`. tree's positions are bounded only through `len src < 1024`, and
   `assume` now narrows at entry.

**The width was the whole gap on json tree, and a controlled experiment showed it** (9 interleaved
rounds, one process each):

| tree, ns/op | hand | `gen` | IR, tables `int` | IR, tables `int16` (forced) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| median | 4,440 | 4,869 | 5,107 | 4,757 |
| against `gen` | | 1.00 | 1.049 | **0.977** |

With the two fixes, the IR proves the widths itself.

**Across the corpus the IR narrows more than the term backend.** Of 78 Go programs, the allocated
element types are:

| | `[]int` | `[]byte` | `[]int16` | `[]uint16` | `[]uint32` | `[]bool` | `[]string` | `[]float64` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| term backend | 22 | 20 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| IR printer | 13 | 20 | 5 | 7 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 2 |

Every new narrowing is in `freq` or `tree`, and each was checked against its program:
- **freq's span table is `uint16`.** Positions lie in [0, 65535] because the program refuses
  inputs of 65,536 bytes or more (`cap-in`), and the IR proves it through the length π on `src`
  where the term analysis declined.
- **tree's ten tables** are bounded through `len src < 1024`. `TreeRun` gives identical answers
  under both printers on 42 inputs, and `TreeMeasure` passes the gauntlet's agreement test.

## 5. Found on the way

- **The limb kernel in IR_P.** Under `-big-repr=limbs` a `big` is a table of limbs, and IR_P's
  containment must read it as the sort it shares with that table, as IR_A already did. Four
  differential builds found it.
- **An `if` in value position is dropped when unread only if it is total.** A loop never is: it may
  not terminate, and dropping it would turn ⊥ into a value (L7's premise).
- **A source `where` is not assumed over a parameter that `PromoteBig` moved above the word.** Its
  operations would no longer mean what they meant in the source.

## 6. Not built

- **Deleting `emit/golang.go`.** It remains as `-printer terms` until the other printers move, so the
  two can be compared on any program.
- **Printers for JavaScript, Java and x86** (step 3).
- **The relational theorems** in the IR domain (monotone steps, trip counts, `x ≤ x·x`: irp3 §2). No
  gauntlet width needs them now.
- **The premise of re-slicing** by anything but an assumption of the same term. A linear fact,
  len X = len Y proven rather than assumed, is the linear domain's, and is not ported.
