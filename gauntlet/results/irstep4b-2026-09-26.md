# IR step 4b: the IR's interval domain proves what the term analysis proves

2026-09-26. ADR 0032's step 4 ports the analyses to the IR, one domain at a time, starting with the
interval domain as a legality checker run in shadow beside the term analysis. The bar is the term
analysis's count: **2,007 of 2,054** integer operations proven inside their target's word. The IR's
domain began at 1,416. **It now proves 2,007 of 2,054, function by function**: in each of the 148
functions of the 528 source × target runs it proves exactly the term analysis's count, never fewer
and never more.

## 1. The measurement

`gen -irproof` lowers a program's residual, runs the IR's domain, and prints both counts, the
operations the IR leaves unproven with their operands' facts, and the term side's. The population is
the term analysis's, matched exactly (2,054 on both sides):
- `add`, `sub`, `mul` and `neg` in the language, counted once each;
- `u64+`, `u64-` and `u64*`, proven inside U;
- **not** a host operation that lowering promoted to IR arithmetic (`js.+`, kept as the statement's
  provenance and counted apart: 25 of 33);
- **not** a value that only feeds an `assume` (a `where` checked at a call, which is specification).

## 2. What closed the gap, in order

Each row is one rule, stated with its theorem in `ir/interval.go` or `ir/trip.go` and summarised in
spec/ir.md §7.1. The count after it is the sweep's.

| rule | literature | IR proven |
|---|---|---:|
| the domain as it stood after step 3 | | 1,416 |
| `assume` through a π: narrowing a π narrows its source | | 1,429 |
| widening with thresholds (the function's constants, k ± 1, 0, the word's ends) | Blanchet et al. 2003 | 1,650 |
| backward propagation through `+ − neg`, `·` by a literal, squaring; the other side of a π | HC4-revise, Benhamou et al. 1999 | 1,675 |
| the arm conditions: a guard and its negation, through `and`, `or`, `not` | | 1,700 |

| the step through an inner loop's result, with its break offsets | loop monotonicity (emit/monotone.go) | 1,905 |
| the dividend bound: \|a / b\| ≤ \|a\| and \|a % b\| < \|a\| for every divisor | | 1,907 |
| the unsigned word's transfers, and `big%-small` | | 1,912 |

| **the threshold phase bounded at 16 rounds, the plain widening after it** | Blanchet et al.'s delay | **2,007** |

**Theorem 3** is the term side's bounded-increment theorem on the IR. A buffer parameter p of a loop
with at most B back edges starts with cells in z. Suppose every store the loop makes into p is either
fresh, in Z, or a read of p plus a step in Δ. Then every cell stays in
[min(z.lo, Zlo) + B·min(Δlo, 0), max(z.hi, Zhi) + B·max(Δhi, 0)].

The reads are of p's value at the iteration's start, so one iteration raises the maximum by at most
max(Δhi, 0), however many stores it makes. That is sharper than the term theorem's T·s. It bounds
freq's run-length count and tree's worklist depth.

**The last 84 were one non-convergence, not a missing rule.** `tree.oro`'s `run` inlines the
tokeniser, so its constants give hundreds of thresholds. The walk's counters climbed them one per
round (31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 42, …) until the 64-round budget ended, and the loop took ⊤: sound, and
useless.

Widening with thresholds does have finite chains, but they are only as short as the threshold set is
sparse. Thresholds now apply for 16 rounds, and the plain widening after that, whose chains are at
most two steps per end. What the jump loses, the descending iterations and the trip bounds recover.
The same run found that an infinite end carried a stale number, so two equal facts could compare
unequal. Ends are canonical now: that cost nothing here, but it is what equality of facts needs.

## 3. Soundness: checked against executions

The local table (every operation against ℤ, exhaustively on small intervals) cannot check a rule that
concludes something about every iteration of a loop. **An execution can**:
- `ir/interp_test.go` is a concrete interpreter of the IR over ℤ (big.Int, never a word), which
  records every value each time it is defined, and a buffer as a snapshot of its cells;
- `TestTheLoopRulesAreSound` generates 280 programs from nine shapes, one per rule, and runs each on
  five inputs. All 280 compile and 1,400 runs are checked; an anti-vacuity guard fails the test if
  fewer compile or run. Every value, and every cell of every buffer, must lie in its fact;
- `TestTreeRunIsContained` runs tree.oro's `run` on its own input, and requires its 416 operations
  proven, which pins the convergence.

**Every rule was shown to fail against a planted fault, 23 in all.** Three plants survived the first
generator, and each named a shape it lacked. Each shape was added and each plant then failed:
- the decreasing rank bound's +1 needed a loop whose trip count is exactly tight, so the decreasing
  shape counts its iterations;
- the inner-loop step's offset, and its monotonicity premise, needed an inner-loop result that moves
  the outer parameter *down* by an exact amount, and one whose inner loop descends.

| rule | planted fault | caught by |
|---|---|---|
| Theorem 2 | each of the three bounds without its +1 | the loop shapes |
| Theorem 3 | a fresh store forgotten; any table's read taken as p's; an increment or decrement one short; a store not collected | the buffer shapes, tree `run` |
| the step Δ | the inner-loop offset one high; monotonicity not required | the tight inner-loop shape |
| the arm conditions | no negation on the false arm; no flip for the right operand; ∧ on the false arm; ∨'s second operand taken as holding | the condition shape, tree `run` |
| backward propagation | `+`'s inverse as `+`; `−`'s right inverse reversed | the condition shape |
| the other side of a π | unflipped | the condition shape |
| the threshold phase | unbounded | tree `run`'s proof count |
| the unsigned word | each of the four transfers as the old identity, or without its wrap check | `TestTheUnsignedTransfersAreTheResidueMap` |

**The unsigned transfers were unsound, and unreachable.** `u64-of` is Go's `uint64(x)`, the residue
map, and the IR took it as the identity: a negative x is x + 2⁶⁴, not x. `u64+` past 2⁶⁴ wraps too.
Only `wordsel` writes these primitives, and only on values both realizations hold, so no program
reached the difference. But a transfer must be sound for every argument its declaration admits. Each
is now the exact image under the residue map, which is the identity on S ∩ U, so no proof moved. The
term side's transfer (`emit/interval.go`, "its conversions are the identity on them") rests on the
same premise, and is retired with the term analyses.

## 4. A policy test became a proof

`TestABufferStillMayNotNarrowOnItsOwnContents` forbade narrowing a buffer whose store is
`(+ (b k) 1)`, because on the term side deciding a width from a buffer's own stores was reasoning in a
circle. It was labelled a policy test: with the guard removed, no counterexample was found.

Theorem 3 is not circular; it is induction on the back edges. Eight increments of one bound every
cell by 8, so the buffer is now a `[]byte`, correctly. The test is restated as
`TestABufferNarrowsOnItsOwnContentsOnlyByAProof`:
- the eight-trip buffer narrows;
- the same increments over 4,000 trips reach 500 and must not be a `[]byte` (it fails when Theorem 3
  ignores the increment);
- the literal control narrows.

## 5. Gates

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| the module's tests | pass |
| the differential suite | pass, on four targets |
| the IR step | 250 of 250 emitted programs lowered and verified; round trip exact |
| proof counts | unchanged: 2,007 of 2,054 operations, 343 of 381 loops (the term analysis still decides legality) |
| emission | **8 outputs changed**, all narrowings from the IR's tighter facts, which Finalize meets with the term analysis's; accepted once |
| compile time | 1.22× the baseline's median, with no compile over the rule |

The 8:
- freq's count tables became `uint16` on Go and `short` on Java, by Theorem 3;
- tree's worklist became `int16` in `measure`, and `run`'s tables became `int8` and `byte`, since
  that function parses a fixed literal and its cells are proven in [−3, 6];
- Java scalars proven inside ±2³¹ became `int`: sieve-java, wc, render, and big-divmod's remainder
  of 10⁸.

**One changed output is a gauntlet benchmark**: program 7's tree walk on Go. Timed serially, with
each binary interleaved over six rounds of 20,000 iterations:

| | median (ns/op) | minimum |
|---|---:|---:|
| the IR before this change | 4,620 | 4,583 |
| the IR now (`int16` worklist) | 4,700 | 4,649 |
| hand-written `TreeFlat` | 4,600 | 4,571 |

1.02× hand-written: at parity. The narrower worklist is about 1.5% slower than the `int` one in all
six rounds. That is consistent, and far below the 15% floor a decision may rest on; it is the `int(…)`
widening on each read. No Java parity benchmark covers sieve-java.

**The check itself hung** on its first run: 53 minutes idle, with every file of the baseline's tree
already extracted. The tar reader stops at the end-of-archive marker, and `git archive` blocked writing
the rest of its record padding into a pipe nobody read. `extractCommit` then waited for git forever.

`cmd/check/paired.go` now drains the pipe before `Wait` on every path. `TestExtractionDrainsItsPipe`
feeds it an archive followed by 1 MiB of padding: it passes, and hangs against the old code.

## 6. Not built, and next

- **The IR's domain is not yet the legality checker.** It runs in shadow in `gen -irproof`, and in
  Finalize, where its facts meet the term analysis's for element widths. Making it decide legality
  (refusing what it cannot prove) and retiring `emit/interval.go` is the next step, now that the
  counts are equal.
- **Termination** (343 of 381 loops) and **the refinement layer** stay on terms, and port after the
  interval domain.
- **The copy-closed joint invariant** (smashfd's other theorem, over several buffers) was not needed.
  Theorem 3 on each buffer, with the other buffers' reads as fresh values bounded by their own facts,
  covers tree's `nodes`/`stk` pair and freq's merge.
- Still queued: a `build`'s size is not obliged to fit `max-len` (irstep3java §3), and JavaScript fills
  a boolean table with 0 (irstep4a §5).
