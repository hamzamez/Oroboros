# IR step 4c: the IR decides legality and modes

2026-09-26. The IR's interval domain proved the term analysis's count, function by function
([irstep4b](irstep4b-2026-09-26.md)). This step makes it **the decision**: spec §7's step from IR_A
to IR_P, item 2. Each counted integer operation is either proven inside its set or not, and that
decides whether the program is legal and each operation's mode.

- `gen` and `build` refuse from `ir.Decide`, and `-checked` takes its traps from it.
- The term selection that renamed unproven operations to checked primitives is no longer read.
- The printers print the decided function (`FromFunc`).

The term analysis still runs, for what is not ported yet: the termination count, `CheckEnsures`,
`MeasureRequires`, `SelectShifts`, `BufferRange`, big promotion and word selection.

## 1. The domain had to be the same lattice first

Equal counts were not enough to hand over the decision. Two of the decision's outputs need interval
ends past int64:
- **`Outside`**: an unproven operation bounded, only not by this word, where the refusal's answer is
  "declare the range";
- **`InU`**: an unproven operation that lies in U = [0, 2⁶⁴), where the answer is to select the
  unsigned word and decide again.

The IR's ends were int64, so the product of two words and the top of U were both +∞.

**The domain is now intervals over ℤ̄ with finite ends in E = (−2¹²⁶, 2¹²⁶)**, the term analysis's
lattice (emit/bound.go). An end a computation takes outside E becomes the infinity on its side. That
is the only approximation the representation makes, and it is sound, since γ only grows.

The end type `ep` is 128-bit two's complement with one bit of headroom, so a sum or a negation of
ends never overflows the representation. Each operation only decides whether its result is still an
end. `TestTheEndIsTheIntegers` checks it against math/big at every word boundary (2³¹, 2⁵³, 2⁶³,
2⁶⁴) and at E's edge; a planted fault in the product's range check fails it.

With exact ends, the unsigned transfers are exact images of the residue map ℤ → ℤ/2⁶⁴:
- `u64-of` of [−3, −1] is [2⁶⁴ − 3, 2⁶⁴ − 1], not U;
- a u64 operation is proven exactly when its interval lies in [0, 2⁶⁴ − 1], the term side's rule,
  where the int64 domain could prove only ≤ 2⁶³ − 1;
- declared ranges are read exactly (`core.IntRangeBig`).

The soundness table samples the new wide domains: U, around 2⁶³ and 2⁶⁴, and near ±2¹²⁶. **Parity
held**: 2,007 of 2,054 on the sweep after the port, with no function short and none over.

## 2. The decision

`ir.Decide(tg, f, checked)` walks the function in program order and, for each counted operation:
- **proven** inside its set (the signed word for the language's `+ − · neg`, U for `u64+ − ·`) is
  `exact`;
- **unproven** is refused, unless `checked`. Then it is `trap` where the primitive declares a
  checked form, the term selection's own rule.

The report carries the counts, the refusal's lines, `Outside` and `InU`. `(*Legality).Refusal(what,
tg)` phrases the error in the term analysis's sentence (`emit.UnboundedText`, one function for both).

**Provenance.** A refusal names the source operation it could not prove. Lowering now keeps the
application each arithmetic statement lowers (`Stmt.Src`), in memory only, like the facts: never
printed, never read by a printer or a pass. Lowering keeps a stack of binder name hints beside its
frame stack, so the provenance is printed with the source's names rather than de Bruijn indices.
It names `out` where the term analysis named its freshened `out3`.

**The pipeline**, in `gen` and `build`:
1. `SelectShifts` rewrites divisions by 2ᵏ on the term first, so the IR decides the term the backend
   prints, once.
2. Lower and decide. If `InU` holds and words are not yet selected, go back to the unshifted term,
   select words and check again. Word selection sees the term it always saw.
3. The backend's `FromFunc` takes the decided function to IR_P, verifies it and prints it. The step to
   IR_P reuses the decision's facts unless a restriction was written since. `-ir` writes a clone taken
   before that step.

## 3. What the first check found

The first full check after moving the decision failed in three ways. Each was a gap the shadow
measurement could not see, and each was closed by a law, not a special case.

**The shadow measured exports only.** `gen -irproof` compiles exported definitions. The differential
cases' `run` and the acceptance programs' `main` go through `build`, inlined at literal inputs with
prints between them, and there the IR proved less: 24 differential cases failed. CLAUDE.md names this
trap ("the emission sweep compiles EXPORTS"), and it was walked into anyway. The gaps, in the order
found:

- **An unreachable arm was analysed as if taken.** `run(0)` leaves its loop on the first test, so the
  body's other arm never runs. Its π is ⊥, yet its `continue` joined `acc + 1` into `acc`, which
  widened to +∞. The law is Theorem E's: a π is its source restricted to the guard, so an empty π, or
  a guard that narrows a value to ⊥, means no execution takes the arm. Every value in it is ⊥, which
  is exact, and its exits contribute nothing. A dead table is ⊥ in every component: without its
  element component, Finalize read "no fact" as ⊤ and could not discharge `read-loop`'s declared
  bytes.
- **A value no execution defines has no step.** With dead arms ⊥, an inner scanner loop in a dead arm
  made the step rule answer ⊤ and spoil the join over the live arms (tree.oro's `run` fell from
  `int8` tables back to `int16`). The base case is that a ⊥ value's differences are the empty set:
  its step is ⊥, the identity of every join and minimum.
- **The step at one continue.** `match`'s `(again 0 0 c)` resets a counter to a literal. The symbolic
  step has no clause for a constant, so nothing ranked the loop. The general bound is
  v − p ∈ fact(v) − fact(p), both taken at that continue, where p is narrowed by the arm's guards:
  0 − [1, 9] = [−9, −1]. Each continue now records its own facts, and the two bounds are met.
- **B = ∞.** In a loop no parameter ranks, a side whose step is zero still keeps its initial end: the
  induction step needs no count. Theorem 3 then bounds a buffer given only fresh values (tree's
  `nodes` in `run`), and Theorem 1 a counter that only rises, from below.
- **The unsigned word's order.** A digit loop over a u64 exits on `u64=` and steps by `u64/`. On U
  both are ℤ's: wordsel emits them only where both operands are in U. So `cond` reads
  `u64< … u64=` as the comparisons they are, `u64/` by a literal is a geometric step, and the
  literal reader sees through `u64-of` of a literal (the identity on S ∩ U).

**The shift selection hid operations from the IR.** `SelectShifts` writes `x >> k` and `x & (2ᵏ − 1)`,
host calls the domain had no transfer for, so every value computed from a split carry was ⊤. The
guard the pipeline then had (decide before and after the rewrite, and require the same verdict) fired
on 14 runs, which is what it was for. The laws, as the term side's F9 states them:
- x & m ∈ [0, m] for m ≥ 0, whatever x is;
- x >> k = ⌊x / 2ᵏ⌋ for x ≥ 0 and k ∈ [0, 62], the hull of four corners;
- a shift by a literal k ≥ 1 is Theorem 2's geometric step with c = 2ᵏ.

**Compile time doubled on freq** (1.86× paired), because the IR analysis ran twice: before and after
the shift selection. Deciding once, after it, and reusing the facts in the step to IR_P restores it:
freq is 1.07× on Go and 1.1× on Java, serially against `4ab3f4e`'s binary. The sweep's median is 0.93×.

**The IR now proves what the term analysis did not.** Three differential cases declared `; checked:`
because the term analysis gave their elements no bound (bounds-2026-09-24): `alloc-copies` and
`map-len` on windows, and `table-alloc` on all four targets. The IR proves every operation in them, so
the declarations are gone and a line in each says why. That was the first item of the 09-24
assessment's "deliberately not next" list: element bounds for `alloc (table …)` and a map's cells. It
came from the laws above, with no work aimed at it.

## 4. Soundness

Each new rule was shown to fail against a planted fault, 16 in all. The two checks from irstep4b grew:
- **the soundness table** gained the mask and the shift, with shift counts to 62, and the endpoint
  arithmetic's own test against math/big;
- **the generated programs** gained a reset shape and an unranked shape; 330 compile, 1,650 runs are
  checked;
- **the interpreter** learnt the unsigned word's primitives (the residue map) and the mask and the
  shift. It runs two programs that `gen -ir` compiled with their selections, u64-digits and runs, and
  requires every value contained and every operation proven.

| rule | planted fault | caught by |
|---|---|---|
| the end type | the product's range check one bit wide | `TestTheEndIsTheIntegers` |
| the mask | the wrong end; a non-negative operand's bound dropped | the soundness table |
| the shift | the low end by the smaller count | the soundness table |
| the step at one continue | operands reversed; a live continue treated as dead | the reset shape |
| B = ∞ | a moving end kept, above and below | tree `run`, the unranked shape |
| the dead arm | when a π, or a guard's narrowing, is a single value | tree `run` |
| the ⊥ step | a live value's step read as ⊥ | tree `run` |
| the u64 order | no negation on the false arm; `u64=` read as ≠ | the u64 program |
| u64 division, the shift as a step | not geometric (a lost proof); a shift read as 2ᵏ⁺³ (B too small) | the u64 and runs programs |
| the threshold phase, the u64 transfers | as in irstep4b | as in irstep4b |

One planted fault taught something about the harness. "A dead continue given live facts" survived,
because it is a *precision* fault: more steps joined can only enlarge a fact. The soundness question
is the opposite one, a live continue treated as dead, and that plant fails.

## 5. What changed

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| the module's tests | pass, the acceptance programs included |
| the differential suite | **pass, on four targets**, with three `; checked:` declarations removed |
| the IR step | 250 of 250 programs lowered and verified |
| proof counts | unchanged: 2,007 of 2,054 operations, 343 of 381 loops |
| outcomes | no program's outcome changed |
| emission | **8 outputs changed**, accepted once |
| compile time | 0.93× the baseline's median |

The 8:
- two refusal texts, `collatz` and `kara/core`: the same lines, the same intervals, in source order
  and with the source's names (`out`, not `out3`);
- narrower representations, by the mask and shift laws:
  - Java scalars `int` rather than `long` in `limbs`, `power` and `limb-subdiv`;
  - `limb-subdiv`'s limbs `[]uint32` rather than `[]int` on Go;
  - `render`'s digits stored as bytes on windows (`movzx`, and the `shl` folded into the offset);
- freq on Java assigning `(int) (0)` to a variable that became `int`: a redundant cast the JIT folds.

No gauntlet benchmark compiles any of these.

## 6. Not built, and next

- **Termination** (343 of 381 loops) still comes from the term analysis, and is the next domain to
  port. After it come the passes that still use the term interval engine: `CheckEnsures`,
  `MeasureRequires` (the refinement layer's second route), `SelectShifts`, `BufferRange`, and the
  representation selections (`PromoteBig`, `SelectWords`).
- `cmd/intervals`, a report of the term analysis, is unchanged.
- `cmd/gen -irproof` now prints the term analysis's count beside the decision's, for as long as the
  term analysis runs.
- **Kept from the term selection, and worth an ADR later**: under `-checked`, an unproven operation
  whose primitive declares no checked form stays `exact`, which wraps. The decision now has one place
  to refuse that instead.
