# IR step 4e: postconditions, buffer ranges and shifts leave the term analysis

2026-09-27. After step 4d, six passes still ran the term interval engine. This step moves the three
that only *read* intervals, or rewrite by a law under an interval premise, onto the IR's facts:
- `CheckEnsures`, an export's postcondition;
- `BufferRange`, a build buffer's element range;
- `SelectShifts`, a division by 2ᵏ as a shift.

Left for the next steps: `MeasureRequires` (the refinement layer's interval route), and the two
representation selections, `PromoteBig` and `SelectWords`.

## 1. The postcondition, as a subset

A postcondition in the constant-bounded fragment denotes a set S for the result: `result ≤ K` is a
half-line, and a conjunction is an intersection (`emit.EnsuresBounds`, which is syntax and no
analysis). The body's result fact R is the join of the function's yields, from the decision's facts.
**The obligation is discharged iff R ⊆ S** (`ir.CheckEnsures`).

Three things were decided while porting it:
- **A postcondition outside the fragment is refused.** The term check passed a relational claim
  (`result ≥ n`) with the note *"propagated and not proven"*. refinements.md §3a says an obligation
  is discharged or the program is refused, and CLAUDE.md records that note's removal elsewhere for
  that reason. No corpus program took the path.
- **A closed integer term is its value.** The reader writes a literal past int64 as arithmetic over
  int64 literals, so `result ≤ 2²⁰⁰` arrived as an application and was called undecided. A closed term
  of `+ − ·` and negation denotes one integer, evaluated exactly in `big.Int`.
- **A bound outside E is read exactly.** A bound past E on its own side is met by every finite end, so
  it becomes E's edge, which still demands a finite end of R. One past E on the far side admits only
  R = ⊥. Reading it as ∞ instead would accept an unbounded result against `result ≤ 2²⁰⁰`, and the
  test's planted fault does exactly that.

The two tests that exercised the old check moved to an external test file (`ensures_ir_test.go`),
keeping what each pinned: a false ranged result is refused and a true one accepted, and it is
*decided*; a bignum's range synthesises no obligation. Two rows were added: the relational refusal,
and the bound past E in both directions.

## 2. The buffer's range: the term factor was redundant

Lowering asked the term analysis for each build buffer's element range and wrote it as the buffer's
declared type, the term factor of Theorem D′'s reduced product. The IR's factor is the class hull:
stores, zero fill, and the loop theorems' bounds. With the term factor removed, **emission is
byte-identical**: the IR factor alone gives every table the representation the product gave. The
refinement layer still reaches the same machinery through its loop invariants (`bufferRangeSeeded`),
so it stays until that layer moves.

## 3. The shift, as a rewrite by a law

For x ∈ [0, 2ʷ) within the word, and c = 2ᵏ with k ≥ 1:

    x div c = x >> k        x rem c = x & (c − 1)

Truncating division is the floor on x ≥ 0, and ⌊x / 2ᵏ⌋ drops x's low k bits, which are the remainder.
w is the target's `shift-width`, the range on which its `>>` and `&` are exact (31 on V8, which
coerces to int32).

`ir.SelectShifts` rewrites after the decision, under its facts, and the facts stay valid: the
division's interval and the shift's are one interval (divIV's corners are shrIV's, and remIV's bound
is andIV's), so the step to IR_P still reuses them. The divisor's literal becomes k or 2ᵏ − 1 in
place, which keeps the value numbering the term route produced. A literal something else reads gets a
new one, and the function is renumbered.

**The decision no longer juggles two terms.** Step 4c ran the term shift pass before the decision and
kept the unshifted term for word selection. Now the decision sees the term as written, and the rewrite
follows it.

**The counts in the notes changed, and the new ones are true.** For `runs.oro` the note said 52
divisions became shifts. The IR rewrites 5, which is the 3 shifts and 2 masks the term route's own IR
contained. The term pass counted a rewrite every time its fixpoint re-evaluated a division: a count is
a measurement too (matchguard-2026-09-24). Every shift count in the outcomes moved for that reason
alone.

**Soundness.** `TestTheShiftRewriteKeepsEveryAnswer` generates programs whose dividends are negative
for some inputs and not for others, and runs each one's IR in the interpreter before and after the
rewrite: 132 rewrites, every input the same answer. For negative x the law fails (−3 div 2 = −1 but
−3 >> 1 = −2), so dropping the sign premise is caught, and so are a mask of 2ᵏ and a shift count one
short. `TestTheShiftWidthIsTheTargets` moved to the external tests and runs the IR's pipeline.

## 4. Gates

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| the module's tests | pass |

**freq on JavaScript** writes `len >> 1` for `Math.trunc(len / 2)` in four places. The IR proves that
table's length below 2³¹, V8's shift width, from what built it; the term pass had no such length fact.
freq is not a benchmark.

**Compile time fell** because the term shift pass ran the whole term interval fixpoint once more per
function. With it gone, `gen` runs no term interval analysis on its default path.
