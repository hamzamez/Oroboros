# IR step 4g: the unsigned word is chosen on the IR, and the checker's integer sort is ℤ

2026-09-27. [ADR 0033](../../docs/decisions/0033-an-integers-representation-is-chosen-on-the-ir.md),
written first. An integer's representation is chosen on the IR by ρ of its fact. The term checker
stops keeping the two word realizations as sorts. That a value lies in U where U is declared, or in S
where `int` is, becomes an obligation the IR's facts discharge or refuse.

## 1. Why the checker had to change

`SelectWords` ran the term interval analysis in its selection mode and rewrote the residual *before*
the term checker, because the checker kept S, U and big as three integer sorts and refused one where
another was declared. With that selection switched off, `u128` was refused with *"an integer literal
is int, but int 0 18446744073709551615 is required"*.

That message states a membership condition, and so does its converse ("the two agree only modulo
2⁶⁴"). Only facts decide membership, and the checker has none. So its sorts were an approximation
that forced representation to be chosen on terms, by the analysis being retired. ADR 0033 splits the
two questions:
- **typing, on terms**: the integer sort is ℤ, and a declared range is a claim;
- **representation, on the IR**: chosen by ρ, checked by the verifier's W5, and every crossing an
  obligation.

## 2. The selection (`ir.SelectWords`)

For each integer value, ρ(fact), with S preferred: U if fact ⊆ U and fact ⊄ S, otherwise S. A
definition that declares one fixes it: a U-typed parameter or host result. A π takes its source's
realization. The type is the representation (ADR 0032), so every integer value's type is written
from the choice, in both directions: unification had typed some values `u64` from a U source whose
facts lie in S.

Then, by ADR 0026's theorem (q : ℤ → ℤ/2⁶⁴ is a ring homomorphism, and S and U are systems of
representatives):
- **+ − ·** compute in their result's realization, with operands converted freely: the result is
  proven in it, so its residue names it;
- **/ % and the orders** compute in a realization holding both operands, where conversion is the
  identity. A quotient in U whose own value is in S is converted back;
- **a value in U against one that may be negative** has no realization holding both. It is refused
  by name, as the term selection refused it, where it used to fail W5 with an internal message;
- **every other crossing is an obligation**: into a declared position (a host argument, an index, a
  length, the function's result), and into a join (a result, a loop variable, a stored value), whose
  incoming fact lies in the receiver's, so it fails only where the receiver is unbounded, and there a
  silent residue would change the integer;
- **conversions cancel**: u64-of ∘ int-of-u64 is the identity on U, since the residue maps are inverse
  bijections.

The selection runs after the decision. When it rewrites, the function is renumbered and decided
again, and each `u64` operation is then proven in U. The term side's `SelectWords` and `DeclaresWord`
are deleted, with the checker's U-versus-`int` refusals, now the IR's with the failing interval.

**W5 is now strict on realizations.** It compared sorts with the checker's relation, which had just
been told they agree. The IR's rule is its own: two word realizations agree only if they are the same.
A plant that fed `int` operands to a `u64+` passed until then, because the interpreter's residue
semantics make that sum correct (the homomorphism), while Go would type-reject it.

## 3. What measuring found

- **`u128`'s `decimal` was a silent wrong answer waiting for an input.** It was exported with no
  signature, so `h` was an unconstrained `int`. It passed `h mod 10¹⁸` to `bits.Div64`, which takes a
  `uint64`, and the term selection converted it by the residue map with no membership check: a
  negative h would have produced the digits of another number. The new obligation refused the library.
  The fix is the declaration: both halves are `(int 0 18446744073709551615)`, as its neighbours
  declare, so membership is an obligation at every call (ADR 0028). The loop then runs in `uint64`
  with fewer conversions.
- **A value passed to a receiver is the receiver's value.** `unicode-utf8`'s counter had head fact
  [0, 2⁶³ − 1] from the trip bound, but its continue argument `(if … (+ c 1) c)` had fact [0, 2⁶³],
  so the selection put it in U and its flow back was refused. A value *defined in* a region that hands
  it to a receiver is, on every execution that defines it, the receiver's value: regions are
  straight-line to their terminator. So its fact meets the receiver's, top-down from the loop's
  parameters into the arms of the `if`s they narrow (`exitsNarrow`). A value defined elsewhere may
  reach other receivers and is left alone.
- **A primitive's `ensures` holds at every call the IR sees.** It is assumed where the primitive's
  `where` was discharged (ADR 0028), and every one was, or the program was refused. The IR's call
  transfer now reads `(= result E)` and its half-lines, with ⟦E⟧ evaluated over the arguments' facts.
  `hex.EncodedLen`'s `(= result (* 2 n))` is what bounds the buffer in `encoding-hex`.

**That last one corrects step 4f.** Its allocation-size obligation was committed after the
differential suite, but not after the acceptance programs, the third population of entry points, and
`encoding-hex` was refused from then until this step. The acceptance programs now run with every
change.

## 4. Soundness

| rule | planted fault | caught by |
|---|---|---|
| order and division in a realization holding both | computed in S whatever the operands | the selection test (interpreter) |
| | computed in U whatever the operands | the same |
| a quotient's realization | always brought back into S | the U-remainder shape |
| the homomorphism's typing | + − · operands always into S | W5, on the step to IR_P |
| no realization holds both | left to fail verification | `TestAnOrderNoRealizationHoldsIsRefused` |
| the exit narrowing | the wrong receiver | the loop shapes |
| | a value defined outside the arm narrowed | `TestTheExitNarrowingKeepsItsPremise` |
| the `ensures` bound | the result on the right, unflipped; `<` read one lower | `TestAnEnsuresBoundsItsResult` |

`TestTheWordSelectionKeepsEveryAnswer` generates 150 programs crossing 2⁶³ with sums, products,
orders, division, remainder, digit loops over U, a remainder that stays in U∖S, and results brought
back into S. It runs each one's IR before and after the selection: the interpreter computes the
language's operations in ℤ and the `u64` primitives by the residue map, so every input must give the
same answer. It also takes the selected function to IR_P, where W5 runs.

Two planted faults were not faults. Cancelling a conversion whatever its direction is structurally
unreachable: a conversion's source always has the other realization. And the exit narrowing without
its premise is rarely false in a loop, because a loop's other receivers are bounded by the same trip
theorems. So the premise is tested on hand-built IR instead.
