# joinjump — ADR 0134's B is not the core's term, 2026-11-19

ADR 0134 decided that an `again` after a nested loop's join point is accepted by Theorem J's
rewrite (B): `(P (fn x̄ k))`, with an `again` of the enclosing loop L in k, becomes P with k placed
at each of P's exits. The walkers then see four forms. That rewrite was not built, because its
premise fails in the core. The step's time limit was one hour of reading, and this was found within
it.

## What failed first: the premise

Theorem J (joinjump-research §2) is proved in System F_J, where a jump names its label, `jump L`.
The core's `again` names no loop: it is the nearest enclosing `iterate`'s back edge. iteration.md
§2 says so, and `emit.jumpsOut` reads it so ("an `again` that is not inside a loop of its own").
When P is a loop, placing k at P's exits places k inside P's body, so every `again` in k becomes
P's back edge, not L's. ADR 0031's test program, copied as B would copy it:

```
loop s = 0, j = 0 {
    j >= n -> s
    else -> loop i = 0 { i >= 3 -> again(s + (i + j), j + 1); else -> again(i + 1) }
}
```

The copied `again(s + (i + j), j + 1)` is now the inner loop's. It is refused here only because the
arities differ (two arguments, one variable). With equal arities it would be accepted and would
compute another program. So E′ is a term of F_J, not of the core, and B cannot be built as
decided. The research's §2 proof is right in F_J. Its step "an `again` of L in k stands in a tail
of E′" assumed a label the core does not have. The research itself missed this.

## What the algebra says instead

iteration.md §2's tail grammar is

```
E ::= []  |  let x = e in E  |  if c E E  |  (p a…) (λx̄. E)
```

Its fourth production holds "because a host call's continuation is second-class: it runs once,
immediately, and never escapes". A producer's continuation, `(P (λx̄. E))` with P a loop or a
`build`, has exactly that property. That is Theorem J's first bullet: P runs one exit, once, on each
path, and the eliminator applies k once. So the grammar the meaning asks for is the fourth
production with its operator widened:

```
E ::= … | (p a…) (λx̄. E)  |  P (λx̄. E)      P a loop or a build, its own `again`s its own
```

That is candidate A, read as the fourth form widened rather than as a fifth form. Its walkers are
the fourth form's: 18 call sites of `multiPrimCall` in six files of `emit/` (check, linearity,
monotone, prodresult, refine, values), plus `ir/lower`'s join. The IR already lowers the join
point as a region with a structured `continue` (research §3). Two other routes keep B's shape,
and each costs the same walkers or more:
- a labelled `again`, `again¹`, the F_J jump: every walker of `again` (28 sites in 20 files) learns
  the depth;
- the flat loop, D, today's 16%.

## Set aside for hamza

ADR 0134's representation is decided on a false premise, so reversing it is a new ADR, and the
choice is hamza's:
1. **A as the fourth form widened** (recommended): the tail grammar's host-call continuation
   generalised to a producer's, one predicate for every walker of that form, a planted fault per
   walker that does not take the widened form (a loop's head missing the join body's state, SCT
   missing its call). The 16% is measured once it is built.
2. **The labelled `again`**: F_J's jump, every walker of `again` changed.
3. **D**: keep the refusal.
