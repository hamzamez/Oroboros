# On ℕ, `/` and `%` are `udiv` and `urem`

Date: 2026-11-11. The standing measure's first owed item after ADR 0102: "the same split owed for
`mul_small`, `qsmall`, `rem_small`, `qrem` and `macrow`" (addsub-2026-11-10). **The split measured
nothing**: the item named the wrong cause. The machine code showed what costs: a quotient by 2³² printed
as `sdiv` and `srem`. The facts prove both operands natural, and on ℕ truncated division is the
unsigned one. One declaration pair and two `realize` lines halve K(ℕ\*)'s product.

## What was measured first: the split

`mul_small` rewritten to the index domain [0, m) ⊔ {m, m + 1}:
- a digit is read by the arm's own guard, with m below the table's length (a path fact);
- the last carry is written at m and the header at m + 1;
- **the size is the carry's**. For a normal a and 1 ≤ k < B, B^(m−1) ≤ a ≤ a·k < B^(m+1), so a·k has m
  digits, or m + 1 exactly when the last carry is not 0, and none when k or a is 0. So the loop tracks
  no top digit.

The new loop is proven, its table still born full (no zero fill), and its answers equal. **Its time did
not change**: 289 ms against 289 for `fact(20,000) % 1000000007`, the product with one remainder.

## What the machine code showed

clang `-O2` inlined the callee (P7, the host's inliner) and printed its loop as 15 instructions per
digit:
- a load, `imul`, `add`;
- then `test`, `cmovs`, `lea`, `and`, `sub` and `sar` for `t % 2³²` and `t / 2³²`.

Those are `srem` and `sdiv`, and the sign fixup is there because LLVM cannot know t ≥ 0. The IR had
proven t ∈ [0, 1152921500311879679]: the bounds tests and the size tracking were never the cost.

## The algebra

On ℕ ∩ S₆₄, for 0 ≤ a < 2⁶³ and b ≥ 1:
- truncation is the floor: a / b = ⌊a/b⌋, and a % b = a mod b;
- an i64 in [0, 2⁶³) has the same bits read signed or unsigned.

So LLVM's `udiv` and `urem` are ℤ's `/` and `%` on this domain, with no sign to fix up, and by 2ᵏ LLVM
lowers them to `lshr` and `and`. Their domain is narrower than `sdiv`'s. ADR 0088 chooses, per instance,
the narrowest realization whose domain the facts prove, so it is enough to state them:
- `targets/llvm/instructions.oro`: `llvm.udiv_nat(a ∈ 0..2^63-1, b ∈ 1..2^63-1)` and `urem_nat`, each
  declared as it is;
- `targets/llvm/llvm.oro`: `realize / = lang.div, llvm.sdiv, llvm.udiv_nat checked lang.div_checked`
  and `realize % = lang.rem, llvm.srem, llvm.urem_nat`;
- llvm.md §5 has the row.

No Go code changed except a planted fault.

## Witness

`TestADivisionInNIsUnsignedAndOnlyThere`:
- `x / k + x % k` with x ∈ 0..1000 takes `udiv` and `urem`;
- with x ∈ −1000..1000 it keeps `sdiv` and `srem`, and agrees with ℤ at −7 and every other sample;
- the interpreter now checks each `udiv` and `urem` against their domain;
- **the planted fault** `PlantRealizeUnasked` chooses a realization without asking its sets, and the
  test sees `udiv` meet −7.

## Measured

Serial, interleaved, 21 rounds, median, two rounds. The machine was 2–3× slower earlier in the day; these
were taken at its usual speed.

| program | before | after | Go, `math/big` | ratio to Go |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fact(20,000) % 1000000007`, the product | 114–119 ms | **64–66** | 45–47 | 2.55 → **1.43** |
| `fact(20,000)`, printed | 293–299 | **242–245** | 48–49 | 6.1 → **5.0** |
| fib(10⁵) | 155–157 | 156–160 | 43–44 | 3.6, unchanged |
| 2,000 quotients | 203–204 | 199–200 | 115–118 | 1.7, unchanged |

- **The product is 0.55× its time**: what is left is the digit's own multiply and add, and 32-bit
  digits.
- **fact's printing is now 73% of it** (about 178 ms against Go's 3). That is render's quadratic
  conversion, owed to Karatsuba and recursive division, and to 64-bit digits (M4).
- **fib adds** and has no quotient.
- **D's quotient digit** goes through `qhat` and its correction, and its time is there.

**The check**: 23 windows emissions changed, accepted, every other target byte-identical, counts
unchanged.
- The programs without big integers differ only in `sdiv`/`srem` → `udiv`/`urem` by a constant: a
  digit's `/ 10` and `% 10`, `expect`'s halves;
- the big-integer programs also take the new `mul_small`.

## What it cost

About two hours, most of it the split that measured nothing. **The lesson is the project's own**:
measure a cause before building for it. Reading the machine code took five minutes.

## What was not built

- **The split for `qsmall`, `rem_small`, `qrem` and `macrow`**: the split measured inside the noise on
  `mul_small`. Their quotients and remainders by 2³², 10⁹ and a word take `udiv` and `urem` now
  wherever their operands are proven natural.
- **`ashr` → `lshr` on ℕ**: the same theorem for `>>`. No measured loop shifts a natural.
- **M4, 64-bit digits**: the product's next factor, with `uadd.with.overflow` and i128.
