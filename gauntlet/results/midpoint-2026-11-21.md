Date: 2026-11-21. ADR 0148. The book's finding 108, then 82, 83, 67, 114, 115. hamza, 2026-11-21:
"yes, I agree. if this is not in the book, include it, it is a good example and a good explainer.

## The law

The midpoint of two signed words, m = ⌊(lo + hi)/2⌋ (truncated), is a word, and the sum on the way to
it is not: lo + hi ∈ [−2⁶⁴, 2⁶⁴ − 2]. Two programs say so, each a piece of algebra.

**The consent.** `sum ∈ Int = lo + hi` claims the sum in ℤ, the rung above the word (ADR 0079), and
`sum / 2 ∈ [−2⁶³, 2⁶³ − 1]` returns to the word by the result's claim. A helper says it by a set
above the word, `(-2^64..2^64)`; its `(Int)` is a claim every value satisfies (finding 116).

**Bloch's form**, `lo + (hi - lo) / 2` under `lo <= hi`, needs no consent:

    d = hi − lo:   lo ≤ hi ⟹ d ≥ 0;   d ≤ (2⁶³ − 1) − (−2⁶³) = 2⁶⁴ − 1      so d ∈ U
    q = d / 2:     d ≥ 0 ⟹ q = ⌊d/2⌋, 2q ≤ d ≤ 2q + 1 ⟹ 0 ≤ q ≤ d < 2⁶⁴, q < 2⁶³
    lo + q:        0 ≤ q ≤ d ⟹ lo ≤ lo + q ≤ lo + d = hi                      so lo + q ∈ S

The interval domain proves none of the last two lines: a box forgets relations. **Moore's theorem**
(*Interval Analysis*, 1966): the natural interval extension of an expression in which each variable
occurs once is its exact range, since each of +, −, a constant's ·, ⌊·/k⌋ is monotone in each
argument and independent arguments reach every corner of the box. So the box is inexact exactly
where an atom repeats (lo, in lo + q and in q) or two atoms are related (the `where`). There, and only
there, the term is a sentence of Presburger arithmetic (decidable, Presburger 1929), its division by
its definition, and the Omega test (Pugh 1991) decides it.

## What was built, and the law each rests on

1. **`Int` at a binder is ℤ, the consent** (`core.ZRange`, `core.AscribedSet`): ℤ written as the
   range with both ends open, which every consumer reads as above the word. Go and the JVM refused
   `sum ∈ Int = lo + hi`, windows accepted it: `"int"` was the word's spelling on a host with a
   bignum. Planted: `PlantZAscribedAsWord`. A helper's `(Int)` result as the consent too was built and
   taken back (below; finding 116).
2. **A `where`'s comparison is a zone entry** (Miné 2001), x − y in rel's half-line, read where a π's
   guard is (`assumeDiff`, `guardDiff`): d = hi − lo ∈ [0, 2⁶⁴ − 1], so U. Planted:
   `PlantAssumedDiffUnnegated` (a comparison assumed false read as holding: `(Nat)` of `hi - lo`
   under `!(lo <= hi)` accepted).
3. **`relate`** (`ir/relate.go`): where an operation's box leaves the word and its term repeats an atom
   or relates two, the term unfolded through + − ·k /k and the unsigned word's ring operations (ℤ's
   on their preimages, the words pass's invariant, which the interval transfers read too), each atom
   bounded by its fact, each pair by its zone entry or π guard, each division by
   x = k·q + m with m's sign x's; "in S₆₄", then "in U", decided by the Omega test. Planted:
   `PlantRelateOff`, `PlantRelateWordsOpaque` (the decision after the words pass reading U's
   operations as atoms), `PlantRelateGuardAnyArm` (a guard read on every arm: `hi - lo` on the arm
   where lo > hi accepted).
4. **A range claim the intervals miss is the logic's** (`emit.DecideRangeClaim`), in both claim
   routes: the midpoint of 0..2⁶² claimed in 0..2⁶² is [0, 3·2⁶¹] by the box. Planted:
   `PlantRangeClaimUnread`.
5. **Presburger's constants are integers of any size** (`Lin.K`, `Constraint.K` `*big.Int`;
   coefficients int64): an elimination's constants are the facts' ends combined, and those include
   the word's own; and an arithmetic obligation the linear fragment fails goes to the logic. Planted:
   `PlantConstantWord`, `PlantObligationLinearOnly`.
6. **A constant past the word, widened, is its Horner form over 10¹⁵** (`ir/big.go`, `spine`), as the
   reader writes a literal past int64: S₆₄'s ends claimed `∈ Int` on JavaScript. Planted:
   `PlantConstantNotSpined`.
7. **The claim's tails pass through an ascription** (`mapTails`): (the S e) is e's value, so a
   result wrapped by its set above the word has its claim at e's tails, a loop's exits among them
   (met first as chapter 18's `cstrlen` refused while helpers were wrapped). **J's printer shows
   ℤ's ascription as its operand** (`jshow`): a message printed `the("int -inf +inf", a + b)`.

Every planted fault fails its test first (`cmd/gen/midpoint_test.go`: `TestIntIsAConsentOnEveryTarget`,
`TestBlochsMidpointIsProven`, `TestTheRelationsAreReadWhereTheyHold`, `TestConstantsPastInt64`).

## What failed first

- **The ensure form proved and the range form did not.** `(m ∈ S₆₄) ensure lo <= m && m <= hi` was
  the logic's and proven once Presburger's constants were exact; the same claim as a range,
  `(-2^63..2^63-1)`, was the intervals' alone. Hence item 4.
- **Proving the claim was not proving the operation.** With the claim proven, `lo + q` itself was
  still unproven, [−2⁶³, 2⁶⁴ − 2]: the box. Hence item 3, which made item 4 unnecessary for this
  program (the narrower claim needs it).
- **The second decision undid the first.** After the words pass `hi − lo` is
  `u64-(u64-of hi, u64-of lo)`, and the decision run on the selected function read it as an atom.
  The unsigned word's ring operations are ℤ's on their preimages where chosen, so `relate` reads
  through them.
- **The relational step cost 8% of the CPU of K(ℕ\*)'s heaviest IR test**, almost all of it building
  terms that relate nothing; and the full IR package passed Go's 10-minute timeout. A structural scan
  first (`mayRelate`, no allocation), the box of a combination of independent atoms before the Omega
  test, and a memo per statement and atom bounds: 1.75%.
- **A helper's `(Int)` as the consent cost every program that calls one.** Wrapped as ℤ's ascription,
  an export's body left the tail (each arm's `return` a join, chapter 6's `both_sides`), and a
  helper's made the whole program name arbitrary precision (`MentionsBig`), so the rung ran
  everywhere a library helper returns `Int`. Its demand puts a value whose fact leaves S into the big
  integer ahead of U: the review run refused three differential cases and an acceptance program
  (`strconv.FormatUint`'s argument, an index, tuples' components), and eleven compiler tests failed.
  Taken back: a helper asks by a bounded set above the word, as before (finding 116).
- **The first review run's windows compiles were 1.5–1.7× the baseline** (crc32, fnv, count, wc,
  ledger, paired against `gen` at 968d1c8): `relate` was 25% of `fnv`'s compile, the Omega test asked
  twice per failing term of the printing library's digit arithmetic, on every one of the function's
  several decisions. One question first, the union of the two words, and the answers kept by the
  sequent's text (`validMemo`, a function of the text): 4.4% of `fnv`, 2.1% of `ledger`, the emitted
  file byte-identical.
- **An obligation at S₆₄'s ends was no linear term**: `bot + n <= top - n` is −2⁶³ − (2⁶³ − 1) + 2n,
  past int64 (finding 115). And on JavaScript the literal 2⁶³ − 1, claimed, had no way into BigInt
  (item 6); then the inlined case met the pairs pass's missing signed reading (finding 114, not
  fixed, the case skips JavaScript).
- **A guard I wrote that nothing could reach**: the operation itself as an atom of its own term,
  bounded by its unfinished fact. Its planted fault could not fail: a term that cannot be unfolded is
  one atom read once, which `related()` turns away. Removed, and the reason is a comment.
