# K(ℕ\*) over B = 2⁶⁴ (M4c steps 2 and 3)

Date: 2026-11-12. [ADR 0106](../../docs/decisions/0106-k-n-star-is-in-base-2-64-the-machines-digit.md), on
ADR 0105's wider ends. **Built and correct on every K(ℕ\*) program, and the product's row is now the
machine's**: `mulq`, `add`, `adc`, unrolled twice, the C loop measured at 0.37 ns per digit
(carrysplit-2026-11-12). **The programs gained less than the row**, and each one's machine code names
why. The cost has moved off the digit and onto the call and the carry.

## The algebra

A digit is a natural of the unsigned word, U₆₄ = [0, 2⁶⁴), and every step is an intermediate of the
double word, read back by its two halves: t & (2⁶⁴ − 1) and t >> 64, which are `rax` and `rdx`.
- **A**: x + y + c < 2⁶⁵, read unsigned.
- **M**: u·v + w + k ≤ 2¹²⁸ − 1, read unsigned (ADR 0105).
- **S**: x − y − b ∈ [−2⁶⁴, 2⁶⁴), read signed, with t & (2⁶⁴ − 1) its digit by 2-adicity.
- **D**: q̂ = ⌊(wₙ·2⁶⁴ + wₙ₋₁) / v_t⌋ by `divq`, whose halves are now exactly wₙ and wₙ₋₁. The
  two-digit lemma holds with C = 2⁶⁴.
- **The quotient digit's correction**, q̂ − b with b ∈ {0, 1, 2}: q̂ ≥ b whenever b > 0 (Theorem B)
  is relational. Split on a constant, it is intervals' alone: for q̂ ≥ 2, q̂ − b ∈ [0, 2⁶⁴); below 2,
  a word clamped at 0, the identity on every execution.

## What was built

- **The double word chooses before the unsigned word**, and claims only what lies outside both words.
  The unsigned word then leaves its operations alone: their operands are naturals extended by `zext`,
  and their results typed by their facts. A reading back whose result lies in U₆₄ is proven where the
  target realizes U.
- **The double word's operands may be naturals of U₆₄**, and `t & m` takes any natural m held in a
  word, 2⁶⁴ − 1 being no literal.
- **A table's elements are ρ of their hull** (ADR 0083 item 5, Theorem D′): the fact of the table after
  a store, which includes the stored value. A hull inside U and outside S holds U₆₄ elements, the same
  `i64` read unsigned. A digit store is the first thing to need it.
- **The call's obligation and a result's claim read a set exactly** (`setEndsExact`): a range past
  int64, or `u64`. A numeral's elements, 0..2⁶⁴ − 1, were read by `IntRange` and would have been
  skipped silently.
- **A double word's πs are typed as their sources** (W6).
- **The library**:
  - the mask `dm` = 2⁶⁴ − 1, the shifts 64, normalization at 2⁶³ (f up to 2⁶³, a natural of U₆₄);
  - B written 2⁶² · 4;
  - every word is one digit in `of`;
  - render by 10¹⁸ over 20·len + 18 places (10¹⁹ is no literal);
  - `bitlen` by 64.
- **Program first**: `u64-mulhi`, the halves of a product of two naturals of U₆₄, past 2¹²⁷, agrees with
  Python on windows.
- **The add-back witness** is constructed anew for B = 2⁶⁴, by the same construction and model.

## Why the programs gained less than the row

- **The product.** Its row is the machine's: Σ sizes ≈ 4.1·10⁷ steps at 0.37 ns, about 15 ms of 62.
  The rest is per call: each `mul-small` result is a fresh heap table of up to 32 KB and the last one
  is freed, 20,000 times. Inlined, with reuse by fit, it is 55 ms. Fit fails too, because each product
  is a digit longer than the table it would reuse, and a region serves only a request it fits
  (ADR 0100). Go grows its backing array geometrically. So the product's gap is **storage**: reuse
  across a call (ADR 0101 step 2, destination passing), and a capacity class that a growing numeral
  fits.
- **The sum.** Its loop keeps the carry as a value, `setb` and `adc $0` into a register every digit,
  where Go's assembly keeps it in the flags with one `adc` per word. LLVM does not carry CF across a
  loop's iterations. Splitting the loop at my into two loops in sequence, to drop its branch, was
  tried: the language cannot write one buffer from two loops in sequence (the first freezes it, plan
  item 9's wall), and the branch it would drop is the predictable one. Reverted.
- **Printing** is still quadratic: chunks of 10¹⁸ over 64-bit digits, about 70 ms. Divide and conquer
  waits on a cheaper D step, which is the same storage and carry.

## What failed first

- **Every callee refused a digit store**: the unsigned word converted every stored value to S. The
  table's element set came from its type, which is a bare `buffer int` when the pass runs. Its facts
  give the hull.
- **D's q̂ − b** had the fact [−2, 2⁶⁴ − 1] under the guard q ≥ b, which relates two variables. The
  constant split fixed it.
- **W6** refused a double word's untyped πs.
- **The reading back into U₆₄** was counted unproven, the decision asking a `>>` for a result in S.

## What was not built

- **Storage for a growing numeral**: destination passing across a call (ADR 0101 step 2) and capacity
  by size class, so the dead region fits the next, one digit longer.
- **The sum's carry in the flags**: LLVM's `llvm.x86.addcarry.64` per digit, or the carry chain as a
  host operation over the digits. Measured first, against Go's `addVV`.
- **Divide-and-conquer printing**, after both.
