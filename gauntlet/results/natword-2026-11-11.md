# K(ℕ\*) over B = 2⁶³ (M4b)

Date: 2026-11-11. M4's second step, [ADR 0104](../../docs/decisions/0104-k-n-star-is-in-base-2-63-the-largest-base-the-decision-proves.md), on the double word built the same day (doubleword-2026-11-11,
ADR 0103). The 2,000-quotient program is now **faster on windows than on Go** (0.83–0.86× `math/big`),
the standing measure's first K(ℕ\*) program to meet it. fib and fact are about halved.

## The algebra

B = 2⁶³ is the largest base whose digits are non-negative words and whose step the decision proves
exactly: Algorithm M's u·v + w + k ≤ (B − 1)² + 2(B − 1) = B² − 1 = 2¹²⁶ − 1, inside
E = (−2¹²⁶, 2¹²⁶). Every digit operation is ℤ's, and one reduction serves all four of Knuth's loops:

  t mod B = t & (B − 1),  ⌊t/B⌋ = t >> 63,

since ℤ's & is 2-adic, for every t a step makes:
- A: x + y + c < 2⁶⁴, in the unsigned word;
- S: x − y − b ∈ [−B, B), a word;
- M and D4: u·v + w + k < B², the double word.

B itself is no word, so it is written 2⁶² · 2, and B − 1 is `bm`.

**The two-digit lemma** (Knuth, TAOCP 2 §4.3.1) is the one new fact the decision needed: for
t = u·B + w with w < B and 0 ≤ u < d,

  t ≤ (d − 1)·B + (B − 1) = d·B − 1 < d·B,  so ⌊t/d⌋ < B.

It is relational: t's upper end over d's lower end is near 2⁶⁴, so intervals cannot see it. It is a
transfer for `/` (`numeralQuotient`, ir/interval.go), applied where u is a π of the guard u < d. It
proves D3's estimate q̂ a digit, which is the precondition of the hardware's 2-by-1 division, x86-64's
`divq` (Go's `bits.Div64`), declared as the triple's operation (`targets/windows/x64.oro`, its domain
`hi < d`).

## What was built

- **`emit/natnum.oro` at B = 2⁶³**:
  - `of` (a word is one digit, −2⁶³ = 0 + 1·B);
  - A, S, M, `mul_small`, D4 and D6 by the one reduction;
  - D3 by the lemma;
  - D8 as ((r_{i+1}·B + r_i) >> s) & (B − 1), a shift of the double word by a word;
  - render by 10¹⁸, 19·len + 18 decimal places;
  - `bitlen` by 63.
- **The double word reads back three more ways** (ADR 0104, amending ADR 0103):
  - `t & m` by a constant mask;
  - a right shift by a word count in [0, 127];
  - a quotient or remainder by a positive word, through the triple's 2-by-1 division, where the
    lemma proves the quotient a digit. Each is typed by its proven range, and W14 reads that range.
- **`mul-small` on K(ℕ\*) takes any natural word**: a·k + c ≤ (B − 1)² + (B − 1).
- **A host shift states its law**: `llvm.u_lshr`'s `ensure r == a >> b`, so a realized unsigned shift
  keeps its fact.
- **The printer extends a natural by zero**, which the IR proves.

## What failed first

- **A digit sum t < 2⁶⁴ reduced by comparison** (`if t > bm { t − bm − 1 }`): the unsigned word took
  t, and its `t − bm` stayed an S₆₄ operation reading t's bits, so its fact ran below −2⁶³. Every
  store's fact was lost, every callee unproven, and ADR 0102 inlined them all. The reduction by mask
  and shift needs no comparison, and is the same in U₆₄ and the double word.
- **ι lost a carry's fact.** `u64>>` is realized by the term `lang.u_shr`, and a call takes no later
  realization, so the inlined `llvm.u_lshr` had only its declared set [0, 2⁶⁴), and a carry t >> 63
  was no longer {0, 1}. The declaration now states its law, which the decision assumes at every call.
- **`Finalize` runs before the double word**, so a quotient by a word had no range for W14 to read.
  The pass types it.
- **The `div-small` witness** (ADR 0102) pinned its old reason. As a function, its quotient by a
  run-time word is now the double word's, unproven without the site's constant divisor. The witness
  is restated for that reason.

## Witnesses

- `TestATwoDigitQuotientNeedsItsTopDigitBelowTheDivisor`: under u < d the quotient is a digit and the
  program verifies; under u ≤ d it is refused. **The planted fault** `PlantQuotientUnasked` drops the
  premise, and the refusal goes.
- The differential cases on four targets, K(ℕ\*)'s among them (`big-fib`, `big-nat-ops`,
  `big-nat-div`, `big-sign`, `big-signed-ops`), and the four programs below: Go's answers on windows.

## Measured

Serial, interleaved, 21 rounds, median, two rounds:

| program | base 2³² | **base 2⁶³** | Go, `math/big` | ratio to Go |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,000 quotients | 192 ms | **95** | 111–115 | 1.7 → **0.83–0.86** |
| fib(10⁵) | 150 | **66–70** | 38–39 | 3.9 → 1.8 |
| fact(20,000), printed | 234–237 | **130–138** | 46–47 | 5.0 → 2.9 |
| fact(20,000) % 1000000007, the product | 61–65 | 62–66 | 41–45 | 1.5, unchanged |

- **D gains most**: its quotient digit, three loops and a correction, does half the steps, and `divq`
  replaces four multiplications by a reciprocal.
- **fib adds**, half the digits.
- **fact's printing**: 18 digits per pass over half the limbs, a quarter of the steps by the count
  (64·19)/(32·9) ≈ 4.2. Measured, the printing went from about 170 to 70 ms.
- **The product did not gain**, and the machine code says why. Its loop is bound by the carry chain's
  latency, not the count of digits, and each 63-bit digit costs `add`, `adc`, then `shld` and `and` to
  split t into ⌊t/B⌋ and t mod B. With B = 2⁶⁴ those two are the halves, `rdx` and `rax`. That is
  ADR 0103's rejected alternative, widening E past 2¹²⁸, now with a price: the product's last 1.5×,
  and the remaining ~1.3× of fib's add-with-carry. Zero extension of the operands (a natural's) was
  tried, and is inside the noise.

**The check**: every windows big-integer program's emission changed, accepted with the compiler and
differential steps passing. Counts are unchanged.

## What was not built

- **B = 2⁶⁴**: E widened to 2²⁵⁴ (four limbs per end, every program's analysis paying), or the
  carry's split as host operations (`mulq`'s halves, `adc`). This is the product's and the sum's
  remaining gap.
- **Karatsuba and recursive division**: fact's printing and its large products are still quadratic.
- **The fixed limbs V_n at base 2⁶³**: they keep 2³²; ADR 0083 item 3 retires them on LLVM once
  measured.
- **ADR 0101 steps 2 and 3**: reuse across a call, and parameter `noalias`.
