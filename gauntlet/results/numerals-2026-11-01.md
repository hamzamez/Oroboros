# numerals-2026-11-01: a numeral is exact (ADR 0086, numbers.md §9 part 2)

Part 2 of step 1 of [ADR 0086](../../docs/decisions/0086-a-number-is-an-element-of-z-tenth-and-a-rounding-is-its-one-lossy-operation.md)
([numbers.md](../../docs/spec/numbers.md) §9). Until now the reader parsed `0.1` with
`strconv.ParseFloat`, so a numeral was rounded to binary64 before the program saw it, and nothing said
so.

## What was built

- **`core/num.go`**: a numeral is its element of N = ℤ[1/10] in canonical form (m, k). The scale k ≥ 0
  is least, so 10 ∤ m when k ≥ 1.
  - It is read by `ParseNum` from J's and the raw reader's tokens: digits, an optional fraction, an
    optional exponent, and in the raw reader a sign.
  - It prints exactly (`0.1`, `2.5`, `1000.0`, `123e-300` past 40 places) and reads back as itself.
  - `DecOfFloat64` gives a double's element exactly (m·2⁻ʲ = m·5ʲ·10⁻ʲ), and `Float64` the nearest
    double, with whether it is the element.
- **The core's kind `KFloat` became `KDec`**, and its field `Float float64` became `Dec *Dec`.
  - Every printer reads the double the numeral denotes, which for every numeral the corpus writes is
    the numeral itself.
  - The IR's text carries the numeral exactly.
- **A numeral with a point or an exponent is a constant declared at sort N** (numbers.md §1, revised
  here). It is declared so even when its value is an integer: `0.0` is the element 0, and only `0` is
  also declared at ℤ. That is order-sorted algebra's (Goguen and Meseguer 1992) declaration of a
  constant above its least sort, and Haskell's `Fractional` against `Num`. So the corpus's `0.0` and
  `3.0` keep their sort, and their emission is unchanged.
- **One refusal** (`ir/lower.go`): a numeral that is not exactly a double, at a double, is refused,
  saying so. Until part 3 brings `round`, the double is sort N's one representation. Tested by
  `TestANumeralOutsideF64IsRefused`, whose premise is computed: 0.5 passes the same position, and 0.1,
  1e-400 and 1e400 are refused.
  - Planted fault: with the rule disabled, the test fails on all three.

| | baseline (`f6431e5`) | after |
|---|---|---|
| emission | 620 runs, 352 emitted, 268 refused, byte-identical | the same, byte-identical |
| integer operations proven | 4,008 of 4,060 | 4,008 of 4,060 |
| loops proven to terminate | 476 of 499 | 476 of 499 |
| compile time | 1.05× the baseline | 1.06× |
| gauntlet, ir, differential | pass | pass |

## What failed first, what it cost

- **The first design canonicalized `2.0` to the integer 2**, as numbers.md §1 had it. Then the corpus's
  `acc = 0.0` became an integer literal, and the IR's unifier, which has no subsorts, would have met an
  integer constant at a double. The repair would have been a defaulting rule for integer literals (Go's
  untyped constants, Haskell's defaulting) and type-directed printing in four printers, for no change of
  meaning. Declaring a pointed numeral at N keeps every value and every byte, and defers ℤ ≤ N to part 3,
  where the first value of N that is not a double needs it.
- **`core/num.go` was written into the tree while the baseline check ran**, and it did not compile until
  the rename. It was moved out before the compiler step reached `core`, and the baseline passed. That is
  "timing among parallel jobs" in a new species: an edit during a measurement.

- **One test pinned the old rounding**: `TestAsmFloatLiteralIsBits` wrote `0.1` on the windows target
  and looked for binary64's bits, 0x3FB999999999999A, so it asserted the reader's silent rounding. It is
  now refused, as it should be. The test now writes the double nearest a tenth exactly,
  `0.1000000000000000055511151231257827021181583404541015625`, and finds the same bits. CLAUDE.md's
  lesson "a test can pin a stale text" again, here a stale value.

## What was not built

- ℤ ≤ N in the checker, and the internal sort `f64` renamed `num`: part 3.
- `round`, `÷`, the decimal grids, the scaled word: part 3.
- Infinity and NaN literals: the printers still have branches for them, and nothing makes one. ADR 0086
  removes them from the language, and the branches go when the host's double is decoded (part 4).
