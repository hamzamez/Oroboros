# φ's first kind: a kept definition's sets as LLVM's `range` attribute

2026-11-17. Item 5 of [assessment-2026-11-16](../../docs/assessment-2026-11-16.md) §5, φ maximal
(hamza, 2026-11-16): every fact the decision proves that LLVM's attribute lattice expresses is
printed, one kind at a time, each a soundness rule with its planted fault, measured against C.

**A printed function's parameters and single result now carry `range(i64 lo, hi + 1)`, the set its
contract states.** On the four programs timed it changes nothing measurable, 0.995–1.019× with
identical code size, so LLVM had already derived these ranges there. The attribute is correct, and
the witness shows LLVM reads it: printed one narrower, the answer at the set's top end changes.

## 1. The algebra

- **The attribute is the contract.** LangRef's `range(iN a, b)` makes a value outside [a, b)
  poison. A function the windows printer prints is a kept definition (ADR 0113) or a library
  instance (ADR 0101). Its parameters' types are its contract's sets S ∩ W:
  - assumed in its body;
  - proven at every call, by the call's marks; where one is not proven, P4 inlines that instance
    (ADR 0102), so no printed call passes an argument outside the set.

  Its result's type is the claim decided at the definition (ADR 0072). So every argument and every
  return lies in lo..hi, and the attribute adds no poison: **[a, b) = [lo, hi + 1)**.
- **Where it says nothing it is omitted**: the whole word, the unsigned word (whose range is
  U₆₄'s), a Boolean, a pointer, a product's result.
- **The ends**: hi = 2⁶³ − 1 makes b wrap to −2⁶³, which LangRef's wrapping ranges read as [lo, 2⁶³).

## 2. The witness

`TestARangeAttributeIsTheContract` (cmd/build, windows): a kept `f(x ∈ 0..10)` whose answer at
x = 10 carries 10⁶, called at 8, 9 and 10:
- **with no attribute** (`PlantNoRangeAttr`) the answers are the same as with it;
- **THE PLANTED FAULT** prints each range one narrower, [lo, hi), and the answer at 10 changes. So
  LLVM reads the attribute, and its end is the set's.

The book's tour (chapter 0) quotes the windows emission of `sum_to(n ∈ 0..65536)`. It now reads
`i64 range(i64 0, 65537) %v0`: the chapter's set, handed to LLVM.

## 4. Found while choosing the next kind: `urem` by a constant, rewritten by LLVM

ADR 0008 asks that the next kind be chosen where LLVM is measured to miss a fact. I measured a loop
of 3·10⁸ steps whose values the decision proves in 32 bits (triple-research.md §3) at **1.42× C's
time on x86_64**. Its IR is C's up to one instruction: our `urem` of a dividend the decision proves
non-negative, against C's `srem`. On ℕ ∩ S₆₄ the two are the same function, so the difference is
the code LLVM makes of it:
- C's `x % 7 == 0` is the divisibility test, a multiply by 7's inverse and a compare;
- ours is the whole remainder, a 64×64 `mulq` and its fixups, then a compare against an induction
  variable. Loop strength reduction rewrote `urem … == 0` before instruction selection, and the
  divisibility fold no longer applied.

The same module with the two `urem`s swapped for `srem` runs at **314 ms (min 311), against 453
(442) with `urem` and C's 319 (314)**: parity with C, seven runs each. ADR 0088 chose `udiv`/`urem`
on ℕ ∩ S₆₄ by measurement (`udiv_nat`/`urem_nat`, the K(ℕ\*) product 0.55× its time, which divides
by a variable). So a rule "the signed realization where the divisor is a constant and the dividend a
proven natural" amends ADR 0088's choice per instance. **Set aside for hamza** with these figures,
not built. The measurement is three processes of one loop, and a decision would want the corpus's
constant divisors measured both ways.
