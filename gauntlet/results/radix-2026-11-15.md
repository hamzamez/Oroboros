# Printing a numeral by a tree of divisions, Algorithm D in place

Date: 2026-11-15. [ADR 0111](../../docs/decisions/0111-printing-is-a-tree-of-divisions-each-node-algorithm-d-in-place.md),
[radix-research.md](../../docs/radix-research.md). hamza: "go ahead with printing" and "go ahead
with the in-place D, always mathematical and algebraic". **`fact(20,000)` on windows goes from 2.77×
Go's time to 1.31×**, its printing about 65 → 14 ms, and printing fib(10⁶) goes 502 → 106 ms. Every
proof count is unchanged (5,710 of 5,764, 649 of 673), and the emission baseline changed only by the
new case.

## The algebra

- Printing is ε_β⁻¹ ∘ ε_B, β = 10¹⁸. With P_j = β^(2^j), x = q·P_j + r splits x's base-β digits in
  two, so the tree of divisions is balanced, a function of x's size alone, and a loop over levels.
- **The slot is Algorithm D's state.** A node u < P_j² ≤ P_j·Bᵏ normalizes to u′ < v·Bᵏ, so u′'s word
  2k is 0 and the quotient has k digits. After each step the window's top word is 0 (the window is
  below v < Bᵏ), so the quotient digit is stored there. The node ends as remainder·2ˢ and quotient,
  side by side, and no step allocates.
- **The sign is the top word**: D4 leaves the window in (−2v, v), negative exactly when its top word
  is not 0, so D6 adds back while it is.
- **q < B^(L−k+2)** for a node of L significant words, so a node's first step is
  jq₀ = min(k − 1, L + 1 − k): the steps above it are of a zero window.

## What was built

- `emit/natnum.oro`:
  - `render` dispatches at 32 digits: `render1`, repeated short division, below; `render2`, the
    tree, from 32;
  - `kpows`, P_0 … P_J by squaring into slots; `kpow` and `ksz`, a power and its size from its slot;
  - `kdlevel`, one level: `kun` the normalized nodes (a rule), `knlen` and `kst` each node's first
    step, the level loop over (node, digit) with D3 (`kq4`), D4 (`kmsubrow`) and D6 (`kaddrow`) on
    one buffer, and `kout` the next level, remainders unnormalized and quotients (a rule);
  - `kdlast`, the last level's 2-by-1 divisions by β; `kdigits`, the level loop; `render2`'s text,
    18 characters a base-β digit, then `numeral`.
- `gauntlet/differential/cases/big-render.oro`: eleven numerals of 32 to 158 digits on four targets,
  each host printing with its own bignum, against Python's text. The shapes are the tree's hard ones:
  10ᵏ (nodes of 0), 10ᵏ ± 1, P₆ itself, β¹²⁸ − 1, 2²⁰⁴⁸ ± 1 at the threshold, powers of 3 and 7, zeros
  inside, and a negative value.
- `cmd/build/radix_test.go` with `emit.PlantTreeNoAddBack`. **The planted fault** drops D6. On random
  digits D6 is needed with probability about 2/B, so the witness computes where it is needed. A node
  c·P_j − 1 normalizes to c·v − 2ˢ, and P_j's low 18·2ʲ bits are 0, so the borrow dies below the
  window's top three words and the last step's q̂ is q + 1. A Python model of the tree with `kq4`'s
  estimate (scratchpad `radix/model.py`) counted 8, 32 and 9 add-backs at inputs 1, 7 and 10, and none
  elsewhere. With D6 dropped, exactly those three go wrong.

## What failed first

- **The library's own D at each node** (`qhat`, `msub`, `fix1`, a window table per quotient digit):
  correct, md5 equal to Go's, and **slower than chunking**, 155 ms against 95 for `fact`. One
  4,000-by-2,000-word division took 16.7 ms in the language. That is what asked for D in place.
- **A rule whose body never yields was read as ⊤** by the interval transfer (`OTabulate`), and a
  rule built in the level loop's arm before that arm's facts arrived put ⊤ into the loop's table,
  where widening kept it. Fixed (⊥, the exact image of an empty body) for the allocating version;
  **reverted**, because the final form masks every read of a level table by its digit's invariant, and
  with the fault planted the library still checks. No program names it, so the analysis did not grow.
- **`%` requires !(2·cw == 0)**: `kout` divides by the slot width, 2·size(P_{j−1}) ≥ 2. The clamp
  w₁ = max(w₀, 1) is the identity and states it.
- **A loop variable in [0, 2⁶⁴ − 1]**: q̂ carried across D6's iterations is the unsigned word, which
  no loop variable may be. Carrying the add-back count c instead (the digit (q̂ − c) mod B, fused)
  needed D6 as a loop after D4's row in one arm, and two loops in sequence on one buffer exceeded the
  reducer's fuel. **What the algebra leaves free is word 2k**: 0 in u′ and below every window, it
  holds q̂, which D6 counts down by (x − 1) mod B.
- **The reducer's fuel, 1 M steps, exceeded** by `render`'s specialization. With 20× the fuel it
  built, and correctly, so this was growth, not divergence. A probe (bisecting the fuel per library
  function) found `kdlevel` alone at 640 k steps and a 66 KB residual, 7× all of `div`. **The cause:
  a guarded read of the live buffer is sequenced into each branch of its guard, continuation and
  all** (`core/reduce.go`'s case-of-case), so D3's three reads made 3³ = 27 copies of the step.
  Clamping the index before an unconditional read did not help (the clamp's `if` is distributed the
  same way). **The fix is a proof**: carry the slot's base sb = j·w₂, so every index is linear.
  sb + w₂ ≤ n, jq ≤ k − 1 and w₂ = 2k + 1 bound the window's top, and k ≥ 2 (P_j ≥ β² > B), as the
  loop's guard, its bottom. `kdlevel` is then 21 k steps and 4.5 KB, and the build 22 s instead of 54.
- **At 65 ms, the tree was 3.5× its own algebra**: 29 M row steps where the arithmetic needs about
  8 M. The tree is sized by the power of two above x's base-β length, so the top node (4,016 words)
  was divided by P₁₂ (3,827 words) as if it filled P₁₂², running k = 3,827 quotient steps for a
  quotient of about 190 words, and every quotient node below inherited the zeros. Starting each node
  at jq₀ (above) took `fact` to 42–46 ms.
- **The planted fault's first prediction was wrong at one input**: the model had 10⁷⁰⁰, and the case
  prints 1 − 10⁷⁰⁰, whose magnitude 10⁷⁰⁰ − 1 is of the add-back's shape. Recomputed, 9 add-backs; the
  witness states three inputs.

## Measured

A quiet machine; serial, interleaved, 21 rounds, whole processes; medians, minima in brackets, ms.
The baseline was built at HEAD (`de44be9`) in a worktree, and every output equals the baseline's
and Go's (fib(10⁶)'s up to the trailing newline Go's program omits):

| program | before | **tree** | Go | ratio to Go |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fact(20,000), printed | 97 (94) | **46 (44)** | 35 (34) | 2.77 → **1.31** |
| fact(20,000), product alone | | 32 (30) | 32 (31) | 1.00 |
| fib(10⁶) by fast doubling, printed | 502 (472) | **106 (102)** | 29 (28) | 17 → **3.7** |
| fib(10⁶) by fast doubling, mod p | | 29 (27) | 16 (15) | 1.8 |

| 2,000 quotients | 78 (76) | 80 (77) | 101 (88) | 0.79 |

- **Printing 20,000!** (4,016 words, 77,338 digits): about 65 → 14 ms, against the C model's 9.8 for
  the same design and Go's 3. The C model's figure was the target. The remaining 1.4× is not
  located: the row is the triple's `mul`/`sbb` chain, so its candidates are the per-node work (the
  rules `kun` and `kout`, each a division by a slot width per element, and the 2-by-1 divisions).
- **Printing fib(10⁶)** (10,850 words): about 473 → 77 ms, against Go's 13. Both are quadratic in
  the tree's D; Go's is recursive division over Karatsuba (Burnikel and Ziegler), subquadratic, so the
  gap grows with size: 5× at 4,000 words, 6× at 10,850.
- **The compile**: 1.00× the baseline on the emission sweep, no suspect confirmed. Building `fact`
  for windows takes 22 s, render's specialization 21 k reduction steps for the level.

## What was not built

- **The subquadratic division at a node**: Newton's reciprocal with Barrett's reduction, or
  Burnikel and Ziegler's recursive division, over Karatsuba. Two products a node at today's 3–4× Go's
  product (kernels-research.md §7) are no better than D, so it follows the postponed kernel work.
- **`nat.div` in place**: the library's Algorithm D still allocates a window per quotient digit
  (the 2,000-quotient program, 0.79× Go, divides 64-word numbers, where it costs little). The slot
  theorem applies there too; no program asks yet.
- **The rule transfer's ⊥**, above: correct, and waiting on a program that needs it.
