# The exactness report names every operation, each by its source

Date: 2026-11-22. hamza, 2026-11-22: "1A then 2A, go ahead always mathematical and algebraic". This is
1A: ADR 0150's report made D_A itself, not its first four; and provenance kept through rewriting.

## The law

The report of a function is the set D_A of its operations held exactly, each named by the source
application it lowers. Two things kept it from being that set:

1. **a sample**: the first four were named and the rest an ellipsis;
2. **a lost provenance**: a statement a pass builds from another (the decimals' scaled subtraction
   and division, derived from a rounding) had no source, and was named by its kind, `sub; div`.

Provenance is preserved by rewriting: a statement a pass derives from s computes part of s's source
operation, so it names s's source, in its own kind: `div in round("int", (1 - 0.9) * 10¹⁸, …)`. It is
kept in a field of its own, `Of`, apart from `Src`: `Src` also says which name a statement lowers, and
`langOp` (ir/big.go) reads it to tell the language's operators from a host's. Set to a rounding's
application, the derived division read as a host operation and left ℤ: provenance in `Src` changed
what was computed, the first thing that failed.

## What was built

1. **The whole list** (ir/exact.go): every operation of D_A named; `PlantReportFirstFour` keeps
   four.
2. **`Of` and `provenance`** (ir/ir.go): the source a rewrite derived a statement from, for a
   diagnostic only; `provenance` reads `Src`, then `Of`. The decimals pass (ir/numbers.go,
   `inheritSrc`) gives each statement it builds, and each statement of the regions it builds, the
   rewritten statement's source; `PlantSrcNotInherited` leaves it empty.
3. **Refusals too** (ir/decide.go, `unprovenLine`): a derived statement's refusal names its source,
   `mul [−2⁵⁴, 2⁵⁴] in round("int", (1 - 0.9) * 10¹⁸, "nearest")` where it said `in mul`.
4. **`TestTheReportNamesEveryOperation`** (cmd/gen), gen's notes read from its standard error: eight
   operations past E named on Go where four and "…" were; chapter 8's `ring` on JavaScript naming
   `sub in round(` and `div in round(`; each planted fault fails first.

## What failed first

- **Provenance in `Src`**: `ring`'s report went from four operations to two, the derived subtraction
  and division gone from D_A, because `langOp` read the rounding's application as a host's name. The
  field `Of` keeps provenance a diagnostic's.
- **Two operations from one rounding read alike** under the rounding's text alone; each is named by
  its kind in it.
