# Output size — 2026-08-13

The oldest unchecked box in the project. Requirement 6 asks for small binaries and footprints,
and until now it had no numbers at all against it.

Two questions: **what is the floor**, and **what does specialization cost per call site** — the
tension [g3](../../docs/derivations/g3-generics.md) left unresolved, since a non-recursive
definition is a rewrite rule and therefore gets copied to every call site.

**Result: on Go the floor dominates absolutely and the tension inverts. On JS gzip erases most
of it. And the crossover where specialization stops paying turns out to be the host's own
inlining budget — the same number that appeared in the performance baseline.**

Reproduce with [`measure.sh`](../size/measure.sh) and [`fat.sh`](../size/fat.sh).

---

## 1. The floor

A Go program that allocates an 8-element slice and reads its length, built with `-ldflags="-s -w"`:

**1,495,552 bytes — 1.43 MB.**

Two hundred specialized call sites of a small loop add **14,848 bytes**, which is **1.0%** of
that.

> **On garbage-collected hosts, requirement 6 is about a runtime we do not control.**

Per target: Go's floor is ~1.43 MB; JS's is effectively zero, because the engine is the
browser's and we ship only source; the JVM's is the JVM. So "small binaries" means three
different things, and on the target where we most influence it — JS — the unit is *bytes over
the wire*, not megabytes on disk.

The requirement as written is really a C and embedded requirement, and it is deferred along with
the C target ([ADR 0004](../../docs/decisions/0004-first-targets.md)).

## 2. Small bodies: specialization is *cheaper*

Marginal cost per call site, small body (one loop, ~5 operations), measured from N=1 to N=200:

| | Go bytes/site | JS raw bytes/site | JS gzip bytes/site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared (one outlined function) | **100** | 24.5 | 2.4 |
| Specialized (a copy per site) | **74** | 89.5 | 2.8 |

On Go, **specialization is 26% cheaper** than sharing — a call site plus an outlined function is
larger than a small inlined loop.

On JS, specialization costs 3.7× more raw but only **1.2× more gzipped**. Full figures at N=200:

| | raw | gzipped | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared | 5,100 | 677 | 7.5× |
| Specialized | 17,994 | 740 | **24×** |

Specialized copies are highly repetitive, so they compress extraordinarily well. **Gzip erases
about 95% of the penalty**, and gzipped size is what a browser transfers.

Caveat worth keeping: raw size still governs **parse time and post-parse memory**. V8 parses
18KB, not 740 bytes. Transfer is not the only cost.

## 3. The crossover is the host's inlining budget

100 call sites, varying the number of operations in the body:

| ops in body | shared | specialized | specialized ÷ shared, over floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +10,240 | +7,680 | **0.75×** |
| 2 | +12,288 | +9,728 | 0.79× |
| 4 | +17,408 | +14,848 | 0.85× |
| **8** | **+5,632** | **+26,112** | **4.64×** |
| 16 | +5,632 | +45,568 | 8.09× |
| 32 | +6,144 | +88,576 | **14.42×** |

The sharp discontinuity between 4 and 8 operations is not ours — **it is Go's inliner giving
up.** Below the budget, Go inlines the shared function into all 100 sites anyway, so "shared"
*is* specialized and the specialized version wins by skipping the outlined copy. Above it, Go
outlines, the shared column goes flat at ~+5,632 bytes regardless of body size, and specialized
grows linearly.

That flat column is Go's cost budget of 80 made visible in size data — the same budget that
appeared in the [performance baseline](baseline-2026-08-13.md), where `Bounds` at cost 83 and
`WordCountReadWrite` at 82 were both rejected.

> **The size crossover and the host's inlining budget are the same number.**

## 4. This resolves g3's open tension

[g3](../../docs/derivations/g3-generics.md) left specialization-versus-binary-size unresolved,
with requirements 5 and 6 pulling opposite ways. They mostly do not:

**Outline above the host's inlining budget; specialize below it.**

Below the budget, specialization is free or better on both axes — the host would have inlined
anyway. Above it, specialization costs up to 14× in size for a performance win the host was
declining to take.

And the budget is *discoverable*: `go build -gcflags="-m -m"` prints each function's cost and the
budget it was measured against. Per [ADR 0008](../../docs/decisions/0008-measurement-over-principle.md),
this is a per-target measurement rather than a principle — V8 and HotSpot have their own
thresholds, and they should be measured, not assumed to match Go's.

## 5. Findings

1. **Go's floor is 1.43 MB**, and 200 specialized call sites add 1.0% of it. On GC'd hosts,
   binary size is a runtime we do not control.
2. **Requirement 6 means three different things** across the initial targets, and is really a C
   and embedded requirement — deferred with that target.
3. **For small bodies, specialization is 26% cheaper than sharing on Go.** The tension inverts.
4. **Gzip erases ~95% of the JS penalty** — 3.7× raw becomes 1.2× gzipped. Raw size still
   governs parse time and memory.
5. **The crossover is the host's inlining budget**, visible as a sharp discontinuity between 4
   and 8 operations, and identical to the budget seen in the performance baseline.
6. **g3's tension resolves to a rule:** outline above the host's budget, specialize below it.

## 6. Not measured

- **Java and dex.** No Android toolchain was exercised; class-file and dex growth are unknown.
- **Real programs.** These are synthetic loops. A real program's size is dominated by its
  libraries and its data, neither of which is modelled here.
- **The uniqueness machinery's own footprint.** RC checks and copy paths
  ([s3](../../docs/derivations/s3-cross-boundary-reuse.md),
  [s4](../../docs/derivations/s4-nesting.md)) add code that none of this measures.
- **V8 and HotSpot inlining budgets**, which finding 6 depends on and which were only measured
  for Go.
