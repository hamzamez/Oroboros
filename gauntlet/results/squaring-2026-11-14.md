# Squaring by polarization in K(ℕ\*)

Date: 2026-11-14. [ADR 0110](../../docs/decisions/0110-a-square-is-the-products-quadratic-form-by-polarization.md),
[karatsuba-research.md §12](../../docs/karatsuba-research.md). **A square costs 0.54 of a product
of its size**, and fib(10⁶) by fast doubling goes 34 → 30 ms. Per operation we are still 3–4× Go's
`math/big`, which this measured and names. Every proof count is unchanged (5,710 of 5,764, 649 of
673).

## The algebra

- The square is the quadratic form of the symmetric bilinear μ: a² = Σ aᵢ²B²ⁱ + 2Σ_{i<j} aᵢaⱼBⁱ⁺ʲ,
  which is m(m + 1)/2 products.
- Karatsuba's square is the tensor power's diagonal: (lo, lo), (lo + hi, lo + hi), (hi, hi) are
  squares, so one operand table goes down and every leaf is a square.
- x·x of one SSA value is the square, a fact of the term (ADR 0036's binders named apart).

## What was built

- `emit/natnum.oro`, each pass one loop and each reading only frozen tables:
  - `sqrm`, the triangle (`sqtri`) then 2T + the diagonal;
  - `ksqr`, Karatsuba on one table, with leaves `ksqtri` then the diagonal pass;
  - `sqr`, the dispatch;
  - `kfin`, the up pass and the numeral, shared with `kmul`.
- `ir/limbs.go`: `big*` with equal operands is planned `sqr` on K(ℕ\*). `emit/limbfunc.go`'s
  signature and `ir/progfunc.go`'s `natCalled` register it.
- `gauntlet/differential/cases/big-sqr.oro`: squares of 1, 2, 3, 63, 64, 65, 128, 129, 200 and 1,000
  digits, of all-ones digits and powers of 3, on four targets, with Python's answers.
- `cmd/build/sqr_test.go`: **the planted fault** drops polarization's factor 2, and every square of
  two digits or more goes wrong; size 1, whose triangle is empty, stays right. Its failing also shows
  that x·x takes `sqr`.

## What failed first

- **W14, "the double word's %1305 leaves its term through (and …)"**, in `nat.sqr`'s IR_P. Dumping
  the IR at the refusal found the `and` in a dead branch: `rd(o, 2i)` tests i < 0, which the facts
  decide false, and the step's continuation had been copied into that arm. A dead region's facts are
  ⊥, so the word selection never typed its mask `u64`.
  - A read testing only the upper end then failed the refinement layer, which cannot prove 2jm + 2i
    ≥ 0, a product.
  - What fixed it is the shape: the triangle a frozen table of its own, read by the diagonal pass.
    The state translation copies a continuation into the arms of a guarded *live-buffer* read; no
    pass reading frozen tables does this. A note for anyone writing a K(ℕ\*) pass.
- **The witness's square `fn sq(x ∈ Nat) (Nat)`**: the set on x was an obligation the refinement
  layer could not discharge at its calls, and the result `Nat` cannot be proven of x·x for an
  unbounded x. That is interval arithmetic's dependency problem, x·x read as independent factors.
  The case squares with no claim. The exact transfer is the image of x ↦ x², not built.
- **A second planted switch, for the selection alone**, would leave every answer right, so no
  witness could fail on it. It was removed, since the factor-2 fault fails only through `sqr`.

## Measured

A quiet machine; serial, interleaved, 21 rounds, whole processes; medians, minima in brackets, ms.
The baseline was built at HEAD (`e5d5f84`, Karatsuba) in a worktree, and every output equals the
baseline's and Go's:

| program | Karatsuba | **squaring** | Go | ratio to Go |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fib(10⁶), fast doubling | 34 (33) | **30 (28)** | 19 (18) | 1.79 → **1.57** |
| the product by a word | 34 (31) | 33 (31) | 35 (33) | 0.96 |
| fact(20,000), printed | 96 (93) | 98 (93) | 38 (37) | 2.57 |
| fib(10⁵) | 38 (37) | 38 (36) | 31 (30) | 1.20 |
| 2,000 quotients | 78 (78) | 78 (77) | 101 (91) | 0.78 |

**One operation**, at about 10,400 digits (`kara/one-*.oro`): 30 squares or products of 3^42000 and
3^42020, less the operands' construction (207 ms), against hand-written `math/big`:

| | ours | Go `math/big` | ours / Go |
|---|---|---|---|
| a square | 11.5 ms | 3.65 ms | 3.1 |
| a product | 21.4 ms | 5.2 ms | 4.1 |
| square / product | **0.54** | 0.70 | |

- **Polarization delivers its algebra in full**: 0.54 against the predicted 0.5 + the diagonal.
- **fast doubling gains less than its products' share predicts** (0.88 against about 0.69): a
  process start is about 10 of its 30 ms, and its sums, allocations and smaller products are the
  rest.
- **The per-operation gap is the leaf row.**
  - The C model of ADR 0109 took 17 ms for a product of this size, so the gap is the design's, not
    the J's.
  - Its leaves are 68% of a product at 0.88 ns a step, a single carry chain two additions deep,
    about 4 cycles a step.
  - Go's leaf row (`addMulVVW`) is assembly at about 0.3 ns, and its leaf count at threshold 40 is
    about ours.

**The compile.** `power.oro` on windows squares, so it compiles `nat.sqr` too, 1.64× paired (1,656 →
2,718 ms), accepted and logged.

## What was not built

- **The leaf row with two carry chains**: o + y·x = o + L + B·H, L and H the low and high halves of
  the digit products. These are two numerals added by independent full adders, which x86's ADX
  gives as two flags (`adcx`, `adox`, with `mulx`). They need the BMI2 and ADX features, which the
  triple x86_64-pc-windows-msvc does not assume, so they are a target fact. It is the next step's
  research, since it governs every product: M, Karatsuba's leaves, the square's triangle and
  `mul_small`.
- **The interval of a square**: above, waiting on a program claiming a square's set.
- **Offsets for the down pass's lo and hi**, and the passes' guarded reads: 33% of a product in the
  C model.
