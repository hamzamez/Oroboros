# A 128-bit integer, written in the language over math/bits and strconv

2026-09-23. Plan item 3 of [assessment-2026-09-23](../../docs/assessment-2026-09-23.md): one program
per two packages. The pair is `math/bits` ([mathbits-2026-09-23](mathbits-2026-09-23.md)) and
`strconv` ([strconv-2026-09-22](strconv-2026-09-22.md)). The program is `lib/num/u128.oro`, a module,
and `examples/u128/factorials.oro`, which uses it. Two differential cases check it on every run:
`u128-fact` and `u128-ops`.

It found **four compiler faults**, all fixed and each pinned by a test that fails against the fault. It
also met **one wall three times**, and that wall needs a design decision (§4).

## 1. The algebra

```
V = [0, 2^128),   ι : U × U → V,   ι(h, l) = h·2^64 + l        a bijection: two digits in base 2^64

add    ι(s) + c·2^128 = ι(a) + ι(b),        c ∈ {0, 1}          Knuth 4.3.1 Algorithm A
mulw   ι(a)·w = ι(p) + k·2^128,  exact iff k = 0               Algorithm M, one-digit multiplier
divw   ι(a) = ι(q)·y + r,   0 ≤ r < y,   y ≥ 1                 short division, 4.3.1 ex. 16
decimal  repeated short division by G = 10^18; the leading group through strconv, the rest as 18 digits
```

`divw`'s second step divides `(r₁, al)` by y. Div64 requires its high word to be below y, and r₁ < y is
a law of Div64's *tuple*, which cannot be stated. So r₁ is taken from **Rem64**, whose
`(ensures (< result y))` can be. That precondition is proven, not guarded.

## 2. What it computes

`factorials` prints n! for 0 ≤ n ≤ 34, then "35 overflows 128 bits". 34! < 2¹²⁸ < 35!, and the
overflow is `mulw`'s flag. All 36 lines are identical to Python's `math.factorial`. The cases pin the
boundaries:

| input | why |
|---|---|
| 20!, 21! | the last in U, and the first with a high digit |
| 25! | the first with three base-10¹⁸ groups |
| 34!, 35 | the last below 2¹²⁸, and the overflow |
| 2·34! | `add`'s top carry is 1, and the sum wraps mod 2¹²⁸ |
| ÷ 999999999999999989 | the largest prime below 10¹⁸, so every remainder is non-trivial and 33!, 34! need the high digit's remainder |

Each case was shown to fail against a planted wrong expectation.

## 3. Four faults, fixed

1. **The n-ary let was missing** (core/reduce.go). A host call with several results, applied to its
   continuation, *is* a let binding each result. The eliminator commutes into it:

   ```
   (((p a…) (fn (x̄) M)) k…)  ⟶  ((p a…) (fn (x̄) (M k…)))      k pure, x̄ fresh
   ```

   It is the let rule the reducer already had, at arity n, under the same conditions: the call still
   runs first and once, and a pure eliminator reorders no effect (ADR 0010). Without it, a function
   returning a tuple built under such a call could not be taken apart by its caller. The first
   spelling of x̄ was `#m1, #m2, …`, which is exactly what `match` names its loop variables
   (read.go). Since `Fn` abstracts every free occurrence of its parameters, that could capture. The
   binders are now `#nl…`, skipping any name free in what they close over, because the counter
   restarts with each Env. No existing emission changed.
   - `TestAnEliminatorCommutesIntoAHostCallsContinuation` fails with the rule removed.
   - `TestAnImpureEliminatorStaysOutside` fails with the purity condition dropped.
   - `TestTheConversionDoesNotCapture` fails without the avoid-set.

2. **A projection lost its representation** (emit/interval.go, `multiPrim`). `((Div64 …) (fn (q r) q))`
   is a `u64` when q is, but the rebuilt term was not marked as one, so it was wrapped in `u64-of`.
   The differential case fails without the fix.

3. **The remainder facts were dead in the refiner** (emit/linear.go). F6–F8 in `lang-facts.oro` bound
   `(% a b)`, and `seedFacts` matched them. But their conclusion was outside the linear fragment,
   because a quotient by a positive literal was an atom and a remainder was not. So nothing was
   assumed. `Div64`'s `hi < y`, with the high word reduced mod y, which is the step of every short
   division, was noted **"propagated, not proven"** and emitted. That is the lesson *"not refused is
   not proven"*, and `decimal`'s own comment claimed the opposite.

   A remainder by a positive literal is now an atom. Quotient and remainder atoms are keyed by
   **what they denote**, (operation, the dividend's linear form, k), not by printed term. In U the
   goal prints `(u64% h (u64-of k))` and the fact's instance `(% h (u64-of k))`, so keyed by print
   they were two unknowns. Division's re-keying changed no existing proof or refusal text.
   `TestARemainderBoundsAPrecondition` covers both spellings and has an anti-vacuity case. It fails
   with the atom removed, and again with the old printed-term key.

4. **A loop that shadows a parameter erased it** (emit/interval.go, `iterate`). `openFresh` keeps a
   binder's spelling when the pass has not bound the name, and parameters are never bound there. So
   in a function of h, `(loop ((h h)) …)` binds h again, and the loop's exit deleted h from the
   environment along with the parameter's premise. The next sweep read h as ⊤. The `elem` and `u64`
   maps already restored what they shadowed; `env` now does too. It was found by compiling `decimal`
   on its own, where the U parameter lost its representation and `(= h 0)` was refused by type.
   **`examples/match/runs.oro` on Go had been refused for it**: its count `c` was unbounded because
   n ∈ [0, 2³²) had been erased. It is now proven and emitted, and its answers match Python's on five
   inputs. `TestAShadowingLoopGivesTheParameterBack` has two witnesses, one on intervals and one
   through `CheckSignatures` where the fault was met, and both fail against the old deletion.

**Proof counts:** 2,368 of 2,419 operations and 348 of 385 loops. The math/bits baseline was 2,361 of
2,413 and 345 of 382. Every existing emission is byte-identical except `runs.oro`.

## 4. The wall, met three times

**No backend emits a tail position inside a multi-result host call's continuation.** That
continuation is an n-ary let, and backends do handle jumps and returns under `let`. It showed up in
three forms:

| where | what is refused | what the program does instead | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| a loop whose next state is `mulw`'s tuple | `again` under a tuple binding (binding.md §7) | projects each component: `((u.mulw h l k) (fn (a b c) a))`, three times | the source calls `mulw` three times per step, so the emitted Go has 6 `Mul64` and 3 `Add64` where 2 and 1 suffice. Whether Go's SSA merges the pure intrinsics is **not measured** |
| the same loop, written as binding.md §7 advised | `(let t (u.mulw …) (again (t …) …))` binds a host call, β will not duplicate it, and the tuple stays a closure: *"a bare abstraction reached the emitter"* | as above | — |
| `add` compiled on its own as an export with a tuple result | *"declares 3 results but its definition does not produce them"*: the `(values …)` sits inside `Add64`'s continuation | nothing. The module is compiled through its callers, where reduction consumes the tuple | the module cannot be checked on its own; `u128-ops` exercises `add` and `divw` from a caller instead |

Floating the binding outward by let-associativity, `(let ((p a) (fn (x̄) T)) (fn (t) B)) ⟶
((p a) (fn (x̄) ((fn (t) B) T)))`, exposes the tuple to β. But it moves the `again` into the
continuation, which is the same wall one step later. It was built and reverted. So was relaxing the
tuple-pattern check by shape: `(f (fn (a b) …))` is also that shape, with a lambda that may run twice
or never, and the relaxed check miscompiled both silently.

**Two further walls, named in mathbits-2026-09-23 and demanded again here:**
- **A law over a tuple's components.** Div64's quotient bound would prove `decimal`'s loop terminates:
  ι(h, l) falls lexicographically by a factor of G, and the second component is Div64's quotient. It
  is one of the 37 unproven loops.
- **The linear fragment's constants are int64.** G is 10¹⁸, not 10¹⁹ (the largest power of ten in U),
  because a literal past int64 is a Horner spine the fragment cannot read, and `hi mod G < G` must be
  proven at the literal.

## 5. Smaller things seen

- **The differential harness builds with `-checked`**, which clears every unproven bound. So
  `(= k n)` as a loop exit bounds nothing in the else-branch (k ≠ n is a disjunction), and it passed
  there while refusing in a plain build. Both cases now say `(>= k n)`. No step sees a case's bounds.
  The harness clears them by design (run.go: most cases carry no signatures), and `cmd/check`'s
  emission step compiles a case without the harness's `main`, so a case with no export emits an empty
  package. A case that should be proven, like these two, is checked only by hand, as here.
- **A pure host call whose results are all discarded is still emitted.** In the second projection,
  `mulw`'s `Add64` feeds nothing. Weakening is legal for a pure term (ADR 0010), so it could be
  dropped.
- **The refiner read `(t (fn …))`, a bound tuple applied to its eliminator, as indexing a table**,
  and noted "index bound propagated, not proven" on a program the emitter then refused. It is
  harmless, but a note about the wrong thing.

## 6. What was not built

- Anything in §4. It is hamza's decision, and it spans all four backends.
- The JVM's and windows' realizations of U. The module is Go-only, and says so.
- Multiplication by a full 128-bit number, and division by one: `mulw` and `divw` take one word.
  Nothing in the program needed them.
