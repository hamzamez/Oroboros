# An `int` is an integer: the build of ADR 0026

2026-09-22. Commits `a9d5df6` … `fae5766`. The decision is
[ADR 0026](../../docs/decisions/0026-an-int-is-an-integer.md), the research
[what-an-int-is.md](../../docs/what-an-int-is.md), the representation measurement
[u64repr-2026-09-22](u64repr-2026-09-22.md). The packages the decision was for are
[strconv-2026-09-22](strconv-2026-09-22.md).

**Before:** one interval, W = ±(2⁵³−1), was the language's `int` on every target. It was
the overlap of three hosts' exact ranges, W(S) = ⋂ Exact(T), and so it would shrink whenever a
target was added. **After:** `int` is ℤ. Each target declares its **word**, the widest interval
its native arithmetic is exact on. Legality is per (program, target), and W(S) is a number
the compiler *reports*.

## 1. What was built, in dependency order

| step | commit | what | check |

| 1 | `a9d5df6` | interval endpoints exact to 2¹²⁶ | byte-identical, compile time 1.01× |
| 2 | `7f3de3d` | the window becomes each target's word | refusal texts only; +2 witnesses |
| 2b | `9668585` | `cmd/portable`: portability reported | byte-identical |
| 3 | `3d5ae83` | host integers are the host's | byte-identical |
| 4 | `9e304cb` | Go realizes U = [0, 2⁶⁴−1] natively | refusal texts; +2 witnesses |
| 5 | `5148421` | `strconv`, `encoding/binary` by hand | byte-identical |

All five steps of `cmd/check` pass after each. **No program in the corpus changed legality.**
The proof counts are the same throughout, 2,361 of 2,413 operations and 345 of 382 loops, as
the research's ±2⁶² experiment predicted: the proofs are about *whether* a value is bounded,
not where the bound sits.

## 2. Exact endpoints (step 1)

`ival`'s endpoints were int64s saturating at 2⁶², so [0, 2⁶⁴−1] was ⊤, and so was
`(/ x 10)` on a host `uint64`. `bnd` (`emit/bound.go`) is a 128-bit two's-complement integer
kept inside (−2¹²⁶, 2¹²⁶). One bit of headroom means a sum or negation of two endpoints
can't overflow, which is the same device the old 2⁶² used, one word up. There are int64 fast
paths, with `math/big` for the rare wide product or quotient.

It is checked against `math/big` on the values where two's complement goes wrong: either side
of 2³¹, 2⁵³, 2⁶², 2⁶³, 2⁶⁴ and 2¹²⁵, plus 400 random values. Three planted bugs were each
caught: a dropped carry, a dropped sign and a logical shift.

**Changing the type let the Go compiler enumerate all ~200 sites.** Two of them were hazards
that saturation had been hiding:
- `isqrt` capped its answer at 2³¹. That was exact only below 2⁶²; with exact endpoints it
  would have been an *unsound* bound, and it is now an exact floor square root.
- A `%d` of an endpoint compiles silently with a struct and prints `{0 5}`. `bnd` implements
  `fmt.Formatter`.

## 3. The word (step 2)

`(repr (int LO HI) word)` is required of every target, and the compiler holds no default:

| target | word |
|---|---|
| go, java, windows, portable-go, portable-java, tutorials | [−2⁶³, 2⁶³−1] |
| js, portable-js | [−(2⁵³−1), 2⁵³−1] |
| **blas** | **[−2³¹, 2³¹−1]**, C's `int` |

**`blas` is the int32 target hamza's question was about**, and it was already in the tree.
Declaring its word moved nothing on any other target.

What asked "inside the window?" now asks it of the word:
- `core.Word` replaces `ExceedsWindow` and the package-level `ValueType`;
- the result ascription moved from module loading to the target's `Env`, where the word is
  known;
- constant folding reads `Env.Word`;
- the fits-test, `MaxLenOf`, `BufferRange` and every representation helper take the target's
  word.

**Folding had to become checked.** Under 2⁵³, a sum of two in-window values couldn't wrap
int64; under Go's word it can. `+`, `−` and `·` are now checked, and so are MinInt64·−1 and
MinInt64/−1, the one product dividing back cannot see (`TestFoldingOnAFullWordIsChecked`,
shown to fail against a dropped check).

**The witness: a program refused on every target, for nothing it did.** A result declared
`(int 0 (pow 2 60))` normalised to `int` in the type checker, while the representation solver
promoted the body to `big` through `ExceedsWindow`. The checker then refused a bignum where an
`int` was required, a contradiction the compiler made with itself. It is now:
- a word on Go, the JVM and windows, where both multiplications are proven and native;
- a `BigInt` on JavaScript, where `n·n` stays a Number and the last multiplication is
  arbitrary precision with the bound enforced.

`word-wide` and `word-wide-native` pin it on all four targets. Two things surfaced on the way:
- **A result range's derived contract is kept apart** (`Sig.ResultRange`). It is a contract
  where the range is a word, and a representation declaration where the range is `big`.
- **`PromoteBig`'s erased term was thrown away when nothing was promoted.** A body folded to a
  literal under a wide range still carried its ascription, and the emitter printed a template
  hole. This was unreachable while folding stopped at 2⁵³.

## 4. Portability, reported (step 2b)

`go run ./cmd/portable SRC` compiles for every target and reports:
- A(P) = {T : T accepts P};
- each refusal's first line;
- the representation each accepting target chose;
- W(A(P)) = ⋂ word_T, the old window, now derived.

`-require T,…` exits 1 unless P is portable to those targets.

```
cube.oro
  go       accepted  gen-cube: 2 of 2 integer operations bounded
  js       refused   gen-cube: 1 of 2 integer operation(s) cannot be proven to stay inside the word of target js, …
  java     accepted  gen-cube: 2 of 2 integer operations bounded
  windows  accepted  gen-cube: 2 of 2 integer operations bounded
portable to go, java, windows — not js
W(go, java, windows) = [-9223372036854775808, 9223372036854775807]
```

A refusal whose operation *is* bounded, just not by this target's word, now says so. It also
names the way out: declare the range, which is arbitrary precision there and a word where one
holds it. `TestTheMeetIsAntitone` checks W(S ∪ {T}) ⊆ W(S) on the target files as they are:
- int64 for Go, the JVM and windows;
- ADR 0012's window, recovered as the meet once JS joins;
- 2³¹ once `blas` does.

## 5. Host integers (step 3)

`hex.EncodedLen` is n ↦ 2n on Go's `int`, so it is exact on **[−2⁶², 2⁶²−1]**. That is Go's
own overflow boundary, asymmetric because two's complement is. The window had demanded
|n| ≤ 2⁵²−1, a precondition Go does not have. `TestEncodedLenIsExactOnGosOwnBoundary` pins
both sides of both edges, and fails against the old precondition.

`go.int64` is a manifest range, and counts derived from `len ≤ 2⁵³−1` were re-derived from
`len ≤ 2⁶³−1`. The tooling checker's planted "result wider than the host" had sat at 2⁵³,
which is now inside Go's `int`, so that plant had stopped testing anything. It moved to 2⁶³.

## 6. The unsigned word (step 4)

**The theorem.** q : ℤ → ℤ/2⁶⁴ is a ring homomorphism, and S = [−2⁶³, 2⁶³−1] and
U = [0, 2⁶⁴−1] are both sets of representatives.
- **`+`, `−`, `·`:** computed with wrapping in either 64-bit type, after the residue map
  (Go's `uint64(x)`, `int(x)`). The result is exact whenever it is proven in a realization.
- **`<`, `=`, `/`, `%`:** order and truncating division don't commute with q, so these are
  computed only in a realization that holds both operands.

`emit/wordsel.go` is a mode of the interval pass:
- it makes every term ρ of its interval: `int` inside S, `u64` inside U and outside S;
- it converts at binders, branches, back edges and host-call arguments, each conversion the
  identity on the values it sees;
- it rewrites a language operation proven in U into the target's `u64` primitive, which
  `targets/go/u64.oro` declares.

**Legality is unchanged in kind.** A *language* operation is proven only in S, so if the pass
didn't run, an operation in U would be refused rather than emitted as a wrapping `int`.

| | |
|---|---|
| correctness | 100,010 values (edges at 2⁶³ and 2⁶⁴−1, and random), against `math/big`, for identity, halving, digit sum and a 32+32-bit join: **all exact** |
| witnesses | `u64-digits` (a declared u64 walked to its digits), `u64-square` (a product computed into U with nothing declared wide) |
| planted | division on the signed residues: `u64-digits` returns `-1 -2 -8 …` where `87 86 89 …` is expected. **The type checker cannot see this bug; only the expected answers can.** |
| parity | the emitted `uint64` digit loop at **0.99×** hand-written Go (19.0 against 19.2 µs over 1,024 values, best of five) |
| termination | size-change sees through the conversions, so a digit loop over a `uint64` is still v ↦ v/10, and its sum is bounded |

**What the compile-time gate caught.** Run on every program, the selection is a whole interval
pass, and it doubled `json-tree`'s compile: **95 → 235 ms serially, 7.81× in the sweep**. The
gate flagged it on the first run. Now it runs when U is **declared** (a syntactic test on
signatures and host calls), or as **one retry** when the legality pass reports an unproven
operation whose interval lies in U. Accepted programs pay nothing: compile time is 1.00× and
nothing is flagged.

## 7. Not built, named

- **The JVM's realization of U**: a `long` holding the residue, with `Long.compareUnsigned` and
  `divideUnsigned`, chosen in u64repr at 60× over `BigInteger`. Until it exists, U is
  `BigInteger` there, and ADR 0019 forbids narrowing `big` back to `int` even where proven.
- **windows' unsigned qword** and **V8's split pair** (4–5× over `BigInt`, not built). U is
  `big` / limbs there.
- **A comparison between a possibly-negative `int` and a value past 2⁶³.** No realization
  holds both, so it is refused by name. A two-part comparison is the day a program needs one.
- **`u64` table elements.**
- **A declared `(portable …)` claim** that the check enforces. ADR 0026 keeps it available as
  an opt-in; `-require` is its command-line form.
- **Signed 64-bit on V8** was not measured, and should be when that rung is built.
